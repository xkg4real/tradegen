## Key question 1 — The cross-section of crypto returns (Liu & Tsyvinski 2021 RFS; Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu 2022 JF)

### Takeaway
Two peer-reviewed papers establish that crypto returns are explained by crypto-specific factors — a value-weighted market factor, a size factor and a 1–4-week momentum factor — built on weekly returns of every CoinMarketCap coin above $1m market cap, 2014–2018; a keyless replication on the current top-50 is feasible on Binance spot klines but is survivorship-biased because keyless CoinGecko history is capped at 365 days.

### Cited Findings
- Liu & Tsyvinski, "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency", *Review of Financial Studies* 34(6), 2021, pp. 2689–2727 — [RFS](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/34/6/2689/5912024); working-paper text at [NBER w24877](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24877). Abstract: cryptocurrencies "have no exposure to most common stock market and macroeconomic factors" nor to currencies and commodities; "there is a strong time-series momentum effect and proxies for investor attention strongly forecast cryptocurrency returns" — [NBER w24877 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf).
- Data in LT 2021: CoinDesk prices; Bitcoin 01/01/2011–05/31/2018, Ripple from 08/04/2013, Ethereum from 08/07/2015 — [NBER w24877 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf).
- LT 2021 magnitudes: weekly mean Bitcoin return 3.79 % with sd 16.64 %; daily and weekly Sharpe ratios about 50 % and 75 % higher than stocks, monthly comparable; a −20 % day ("disaster") occurs with probability 0.5 %, a +20 % day with almost 1 % — [NBER w24877 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf).
- LT 2021 time-series momentum: a one-standard-deviation increase in this week's Bitcoin return predicts +3.16, +3.66, +3.49 and +1.50 % at the 1-, 2-, 3- and 4-week horizons; at the daily level a 1-sd increase in today's return predicts +0.33 % tomorrow; top-quintile weeks earn 11.22 %/week (Sharpe 0.45) versus 2.60 %/week (Sharpe 0.19) for the bottom quintile; the effect is weaker for Ethereum — [NBER w24877 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf).
- LT 2021 attention: a 1-sd rise in Google searches for "Bitcoin" predicts +1.84 % and +2.30 % at 1 and 2 weeks; a 1-sd rise in the ratio of "Bitcoin hack" to "Bitcoin" searches predicts −2.75 % the next week; realised volatility does not predict returns — [NBER w24877 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf).
- Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu, "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency", *Journal of Finance* 77(2), 2022, pp. 1133–1177 — [JF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13119); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:77:y:2022:i:2:p:1133-1177); working-paper text at [NBER w25882](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882).
- LTW universe: "all of the coins with market capitalizations above one million dollars and their returns from the beginning of 2014 to the end of 2018. The number of such coins grew from 109 in 2014 to 1,583 in 2018"; source CoinMarketCap (which lists defunct coins, "alleviating concerns about survivorship bias"); weekly returns are built from daily closes with the year divided into 52 weeks — [NBER w25882 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf).
- LTW market factor: CMKT = value-weighted return of all coins minus the one-month T-bill; the coin market index averaged 1.3 % per week (sd 0.117), Bitcoin 1.2 % (sd 0.114), Ethereum 4.6 % (sd 0.241) — [NBER w25882 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf).
- LTW factor construction (Fama–French style): each week coins are split by market cap into bottom 30 % / middle 40 % / top 30 %, value-weighted portfolios are formed, and CSMB = small minus big; CMOM uses three-week momentum, same 30/40/30 split, value-weighted, top minus bottom; "All strategies are rebalanced weekly" — [NBER w25882 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf).
- LTW premia (quintile long-short, weekly): momentum 2.7 % (1-week), 3.3 % (2-week), 4.1 % (3-week), 2.5 % (4-week); the 8-, 16-, 50- and 100-week momentum strategies are not significant; market-cap quintile 5−1 = −3.4 %/week (t = −2.56); size-type sorts (market cap, price, max price, dollar volume) earn about 3–4 %/week — [NBER w25882 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf).
- LTW result: nine characteristic strategies "form successful long-short strategies", and "none of the alphas of the nine strategies remains statistically significant" after the three-factor model; CSMB absorbs the size-type strategies and CMOM the 2–4-week momentum strategies — [NBER w25882 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf); [NBER abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882).
- Keyless data for a replication (live probe): CoinGecko `GET https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/markets?vs_currency=usd&order=market_cap_desc&per_page=250&page=1&price_change_percentage=7d,30d` returns 250 rows with `market_cap`, `total_volume`, `circulating_supply`, `price_change_percentage_7d_in_currency`, `price_change_percentage_30d_in_currency`, `ath`, `ath_date` — [CoinGecko API](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/markets). `GET /coins/{id}/market_chart?vs_currency=usd&days=365` returns 366 daily points (prices, market_caps, total_volumes); `days=400` and a 2024 `market_chart/range` window both return HTTP 401, error_code 10012 "Your request exceeds the allowed time range. Public API user…" — [CoinGecko API](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/bitcoin/market_chart). Granularity is automatic: 1 day → 5-minute, 2–90 days → hourly (a 90-day call returned 2,161 points), >90 days → daily at 00:00 UTC; cache 30 s — [CoinGecko docs](https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/coins-id-market-chart).
- Binance spot `GET https://api.binance.com/api/v3/klines?symbol=BTCUSDT&interval=1d&limit=1000`: `limit` default 500, max 1000, request weight 2, no authentication — [Binance spot docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/binance-spot-api-docs/rest-api/market-data-endpoints); BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT daily klines begin 2017-08-17 (live probe) — [Binance API](https://api.binance.com/api/v3/klines).
- Upbit `GET https://api.upbit.com/v1/candles/days?market=KRW-BTC&count=200&to=<ISO8601>`: `count` max 200, paging backwards with `to`, no authentication, "Up to 10 calls per second… shared within the exchange 'candle' group" — [Upbit global docs](https://global-docs.upbit.com/reference/list-candles-days); KRW-BTC and KRW-ETH daily candles begin 2017-09-25 UTC, the response header on the Korean endpoint reads `Remaining-Req: group=candles; min=600; sec=9`, and `/v1/market/all` lists 289 KRW pairs (live probe) — [Upbit API](https://api.upbit.com/v1/candles/days).

### Inferences
- A faithful LTW replication needs weekly value-weighted portfolios over a broad, point-in-time universe. Keyless, the app can only get (a) the *current* top-N from CoinGecko and (b) full price history from Binance spot klines for those names; that is a survivorship-biased universe, so the replication should be labelled "top-N as of today" and restricted to reporting factor *loadings* and momentum *sorts*, not the size premium (the size premium is where survivorship bias inflates the most).
- Market caps for weighting can be approximated as `circulating_supply × price` with today's supply, or taken from `market_chart.market_caps` for the last 365 days; beyond one year the weights must be price-based (a documented approximation).
- The relevant momentum horizon for crypto is weeks, not months: LTW's 8-week-and-longer sorts are insignificant, and LT 2021's daily autocorrelation is one day. QUANTGEN's Signals page (composite computed at close t, earning from t+1) already has the right timing convention.
- LT 2021's "no exposure to equity/macro factors" is a 2011–2018 result; the IMF work in Key question 6 shows it broke after 2020, so factor loadings should be estimated on rolling windows.

### Gaps
- The published JF/RFS versions may differ in sample end or magnitudes from the NBER texts quoted here; the journal PDFs were not accessible.
- No keyless source of *historical* market-cap rankings was found; point-in-time universes require a paid CoinGecko plan or an accumulated local snapshot.
- Grobys & Sapkota (2019, Economics Letters, cryptocurrency momentum) was not verified — the search budget ran out before the query; UNVERIFIED and not relied on.

## Key question 2 — Time-series momentum, weekend seasonality, ETH/BTC mean reversion

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed evidence supports short-horizon (1–4-week) time-series momentum and moving-average signals in Bitcoin, with the effect concentrated at horizons under two months and decaying with costs; the weekend effect is a volume effect with no persistent return anomaly; no peer-reviewed evidence for mean reversion in the ETH/BTC ratio was found.

### Cited Findings
- Time-series momentum at 1–4 weeks and one day: see the LT 2021 magnitudes above — [NBER w24877 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf).
- Longer lookbacks: 8-, 16-, 50-, 100-week cross-sectional momentum "are not statistically significant" — [NBER w25882 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf).
- Detzel, Liu, Strauss, Zhou & Zhu, "Learning and predictability via technical analysis: Evidence from bitcoin and stocks with hard-to-value fundamentals", *Financial Management* (2021): "ratios of prices to their moving averages forecast daily Bitcoin returns in- and out-of sample" and MA strategies "generate an economically significant alpha and Sharpe ratio gains relative to a buy-and-hold position" — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/fima.12310).
- Weekend/intraday seasonality: Baur, Cahill, Godfrey & Liu, "Bitcoin time-of-day, day-of-week and month-of-year effects in returns and trading volume", *Finance Research Letters* 31 (2019), pp. 78–92: with >15 million observations from seven continuously-traded exchanges they find "time-specific anomalies in returns but no persistent effects across time", and persistently lower trading activity in local evening hours and on weekends — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612319301710); [SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=3088472).
- Data for hourly seasonality: Binance futures `GET https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/klines?symbol=BTCUSDT&interval=1h&limit=1500` returns 1,500 bars per call (`limit` max 1500), BTCUSDT perpetual klines begin 2019-09-08 (live probe) — [Binance futures docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Get-Funding-Rate-History); Upbit 60-minute candles (`/v1/candles/minutes/60`) reach back to at least 2017-10-06 (live probe) — [Upbit API](https://api.upbit.com/v1/candles/minutes/60); Yahoo `BTC-USD` hourly bars via yfinance are limited to the last 730 days (17,480 rows from 2024-09-25), daily bars from 2014-09-17 (4,391 rows) and `ETH-USD` from 2017-11-09 (live probe) — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BTC-USD/history).

### Inferences
- For a daily app the natural signals are (i) sign of the trailing 1–4-week return and (ii) price / MA(n) − 1 for n in {10, 20, 50, 100, 200}, both computed at the 00:00 UTC close and earning from the next close. On Upbit the daily candle boundary is 09:00 KST = 00:00 UTC (`candle_date_time_kst` reads 09:00 on every probe row), so Upbit and Binance daily closes align without shifting.
- Because the documented edge is at one to four weeks and decays beyond, a 12-month lookback (the equity TSMOM convention) has no crypto-specific support in these papers; the app should test 1–12-month lookbacks as an explicit robustness sweep and expect the long ones to fail.
- The Baur et al. result means a weekend *return* filter should be pre-registered to fail; what is robust is lower weekend volume, which raises weekend spread and slippage — a cost input, not a signal.

### Gaps
- ETH/BTC ratio mean reversion: no peer-reviewed source was located (the search budget was exhausted before the query could run). Treat as a hypothesis to be tested with the 계량2 unit-root chapter, not as evidence.
- Practitioner TSMOM studies (1–12-month lookbacks) were not verified in this session.

## Key question 3 — The kimchi premium and cross-exchange arbitrage (Makarov & Schoar 2020 JFE; Choi, Lehar & Stauffer)

### Takeaway
Bitcoin traded on average 4.7 % (Jan 2016–Feb 2018) above the US price in Korea, over 15 % on average in Dec 2017–Feb 2018 with days above 40–54 %, and the deviation is sustained by Korean capital controls (a USD 50,000 annual overseas-remittance ceiling and one-way frictions) plus blockchain transfer risk; the premium is computable daily and keylessly from Upbit KRW-BTC, Binance BTCUSDT and FRED DEXKOUS or Yahoo KRW=X, and today it reads about −0.2 %.

### Cited Findings
- Makarov & Schoar, "Trading and Arbitrage in Cryptocurrency Markets", *Journal of Financial Economics* 135(2), 2020, pp. 293–319 — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v135y2020i2p293-319.html); full text [LSE Research Online PDF](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/100409/1/Cryptocurrency_Markets_JFE_final_v4.pdf).
- Sample and venues: tick data for bitcoin, ether and ripple across 34 exchanges in 19 locations (15 most liquid analysed), January 2017 to end-February 2018 — [CFA Digest summary](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2020/10/dig-v50-n10-4); [LSE PDF](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/100409/1/Cryptocurrency_Markets_JFE_final_v4.pdf).
- Korea premium size: "the daily average price ratio between the US and Korea from December 2017 until the beginning of February 2018 was more than 15% and reached 40% for several days"; Japan about 10 %, Europe about 3 %; within-country deviations "typically do not exceed 1%"; potential arbitrage profit "often more than $75 million" per day and "a minimum of $2 billion" over Dec 2017–Feb 2018 — [LSE PDF](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/100409/1/Cryptocurrency_Markets_JFE_final_v4.pdf).
- Persistence and mechanism: deviations "often persist for several hours, and, in some instances, days and weeks"; arbitrage capital "can become 'stuck' within a country" when profits cannot be repatriated; the "arbitrage index" is the maximum over exchanges of the minute VWAP divided by the minimum; "buying pressure" is the deviation of the US log price from its Hodrick–Prescott trend, and countries with higher premia have a higher "bitcoin beta" to it; a common component of signed volume "explains 80% of bitcoin returns" — [LSE PDF](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/100409/1/Cryptocurrency_Markets_JFE_final_v4.pdf).
- Choi, Lehar & Stauffer, "Bitcoin Microstructure and the Kimchi Premium" — [SSRN 3189051](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3189051) (page returned 403 in this session; the search snippet of that page reports an average premium of 2.27 % over January 2016–January 2020); the May 2019 seminar version (SNB) reports "Between January 2016 and February 2018 the average Kimchi premium was 4.73% but it reached levels as high as 54.48% in January 2018" — [SNB seminar PDF](https://www.snb.ch/dam/jcr:474400ba-255b-49a4-b2b8-5e3626719730/sem_2019_05_31_lehar.n.pdf). A 2026 draft exists at [people.ucalgary.ca/~alehar/KimchiPremium26.pdf](https://people.ucalgary.ca/~alehar/KimchiPremium26.pdf) (403 in this session). Journal venue: UNVERIFIED (not confirmed as published).
- Premium formula (CLS): premium = (KRW BTC price converted to USD) / (USD BTC price) − 1, USD price the mean of Bitstamp trades that day, KRW price from Korbit, FX from the OANDA daily average — [SNB seminar PDF](https://www.snb.ch/dam/jcr:474400ba-255b-49a4-b2b8-5e3626719730/sem_2019_05_31_lehar.n.pdf).
- CLS mechanism: premia are "positively related to transaction costs, confirmation time in the blockchain, and to Bitcoin price volatility"; bitcoin trades higher "in countries with lower financial freedom"; capital controls are asymmetric ("it is easier to move funds to Korea than the other way around") so the premium is positive on average — [SNB seminar PDF](https://www.snb.ch/dam/jcr:474400ba-255b-49a4-b2b8-5e3626719730/sem_2019_05_31_lehar.n.pdf).
- Korean capital-control numbers (CLS): overseas remittances are capped so that "The total maximum is limited to 50,000 USD a year through different institutions", and overseas card purchases are "limited to 10,000 USD per year" with customs treatment as a commodity purchase — [SNB seminar PDF](https://www.snb.ch/dam/jcr:474400ba-255b-49a4-b2b8-5e3626719730/sem_2019_05_31_lehar.n.pdf). The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act "caps annual overseas remittances for individuals" — [The Block explainer](https://www.theblock.co/learn/251472/what-is-the-kimchi-premium).
- Travel Rule (2026): on 11 August 2026 the Cabinet approved removing the KRW 1 million Travel Rule threshold so information-sharing applies to all transfers between registered VASPs, with new counterparty-risk restrictions on transfers to overseas exchanges and personal wallets; the transfer provisions take effect six months after promulgation, VASP registration provisions on 20 August 2026 — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/south-korea-travel-rule-threshold-crypto-transfers); the FIU cited a user who split KRW 200 million of USDT into 216 sub-threshold withdrawals, and full application starts 20 February next year — [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/south-korea-scraps-1m-won-crypto-travel-rule-threshold/).
- FX series: FRED `DEXKOUS` via `https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=DEXKOUS` runs from 1981-04-13 and on 2026-09-24 ended at 2026-09-18 (about one week of publication lag) — [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DEXKOUS); Yahoo `KRW=X` daily runs from 2003-12-01 to the current day (live probe) — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/KRW%3DX/history).
- Live computation (probe, 2026-09-24 daily closes): Upbit KRW-BTC 115,146,000; Binance BTCUSDT 84,385; USD/KRW 1,367.75 → premium −0.24 % — [Upbit API](https://api.upbit.com/v1/candles/days), [Binance API](https://api.binance.com/api/v3/klines).

### Inferences
- Daily premium series for QUANTGEN: p_t = P^{Upbit}_{KRW-BTC,t} / (P^{Binance}_{BTCUSDT,t} × S^{USDKRW}_t) − 1, with Upbit and Binance closes both at 00:00 UTC and S from Yahoo `KRW=X` (same-day) or FRED `DEXKOUS` (noon-NY fixing, lagged a week — use it for history, Yahoo for the live tile). History is available from 2017-09-25 (Upbit listing) — about nine years, including the Dec 2017–Jan 2018 spike Makarov–Schoar and CLS document.
- Why it is a signal and not a trade for a Korean resident: the round trip needs won → USD abroad → BTC → Upbit → won; the outbound leg is bounded by the FETA remittance ceiling (USD 50,000/year in CLS's account), the inbound crypto leg now falls under the full Travel Rule with counterparty screening of overseas exchanges, and 2027 taxation (Key question 8) applies to the gain. The app should compute the premium, its z-score, and its ADF/KPSS statistics, and use it as a Korean-retail-sentiment reading — a positive premium coincides with the "buying pressure" episodes in Makarov–Schoar.
- Unit-root design (계량2 textbook, ch. 5 "Nonstationary Processes", §5.3 Unit Root Tests: 5.3.1 Dickey–Fuller, 5.3.2 ADF, 5.3.4 breaks, 5.3.5 stationarity-null test; ch. 6 Cointegration for the KRW and USD price levels): the premium should be stationary if arbitrage works at all; a persistent level shift after the 2018 real-name-account rules or the 2026 Travel Rule change is the structural-break case in §5.3.4.

### Gaps
- The current (2020s) size of the premium is not documented by a peer-reviewed source here; the 2.27 % 2016–2020 average is from a search snippet of the SSRN page, not the page itself.
- Whether the premium *predicts* Bitcoin returns (rather than coinciding with buying pressure) is not established in the sources read; the pre-registered test below is the app's own.
- The exact 2026 FETA remittance ceiling for individuals was not verified against the statute; the USD 50,000 figure is CLS's 2019 description.

## Key question 4 — Perpetual funding carry and cash-and-carry basis (BIS "Crypto carry"; He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter)

### Takeaway
Crypto carry (futures minus spot, or the perpetual funding rate) averaged above 10 % p.a. and reached 40–60 % p.a.; high carry predicts price crashes, and a funding/basis arbitrage earns Sharpe ratios above 3 even at retail costs in the perpetual-futures paper — but the position is exposed to margin spikes, liquidation and exchange failure, and Binance's full 8-hourly funding history since 2019-09-10 is keyless while open-interest history is only 30 days.

### Cited Findings
- Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov, "Crypto carry", BIS Working Paper No 1087, April 2023 — [BIS page](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1087-crypto-carry); [BIS PDF](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf); [RePEc abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/bisbiswps/1087.htm).
- Definition and size: carry is "the difference between futures and spot prices"; average carry "above 10% annually", "up to 60% p.a."; the abstract says "sometimes exceeding 40% per annum, with significant variation over time" — [BIS PDF](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf); [RePEc abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/bisbiswps/1087.htm).
- Predictive sign and mechanism: "a high crypto carry predicts future price crashes"; increases in carry go with a rising "price of crash risk insurance"; the pattern is attributed to a volatile convenience yield from trend-chasing smaller investors seeking leverage, against scarce arbitrage capital, and cash-and-carry "is risky due to spikes in margins and liquidations amid drawdowns" — [BIS PDF](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf); [CEPR VoxEU summary](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/crypto-carry-market-segmentation-and-price-distortions-digital-asset-markets).
- He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures" (first draft Dec 2022; the arXiv version read is dated September 2026) — [arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888); [SSRN 4301150](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4301150).
- Mechanism: "Typically, the funding rate is paid every eight hours and approximately equals the average futures-spot spread over the preceding eight hours"; the "clamp": near parity the funding rate is fixed at a small positive value, and beyond a gap it rises linearly with the gap — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06888).
- No-arbitrage price (Proposition 1): F_t = (1 − Φ^{-1}(r))^{-1} S_t, where r is the interest rate and Φ the funding-rate function of the premium; the futures price "is proportional to the spot price… with a constant of proportionality determined by the interaction of interest rates and the exchange's funding rate mechanism" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06888).
- Empirics: deviations "are larger than in traditional currency markets, comove across currencies, and diminish over time"; the random-maturity arbitrage on Bitcoin perpetuals earns a Sharpe ratio of 3.35 "under high trading costs typical of retail investors" and 11.65 for fee-free market makers (3.27 and 10.46 after effective spreads); "price convergence plays a dominant role in total trading returns, while funding rate payments have a more minor role, which seems to diminish over time"; Bybit's effective spread exceeded Binance's "by a factor ranging from two to ten"; median perpetual volume in 2022 was $101.9 billion per day, "about 2× to 3× the total spot trading volume" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06888).
- Binance funding endpoint: `GET https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/fundingRate?symbol=BTCUSDT&startTime=<ms>&endTime=<ms>&limit=1000`; the docs give `limit` default 500 / max 1000, ascending order, and a rate limit of 500 requests per 5 minutes per IP shared with `/fapi/v1/fundingInfo` — [Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Get-Funding-Rate-History); [Binance dev forum on the limit](https://dev.binance.vision/t/request-limit-of-endpoint-fapi-v1-fundingrate/21209).
- Funding history depth (live probe): with an explicit window the endpoint returns BTCUSDT funding from 2019-09-10 (first value 0.0001 = 1 bp per 8 h), ETHUSDT from 2019-11-27, 1,000 rows per call (about 333 days); without a window it returned the 500 most recent rows (2026-04-11 to 2026-09-24); `startTime=0` is ignored — [Binance API](https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/fundingRate).

## Key question 5 — Volatility: GARCH on Bitcoin, realised vol, DVOL, and the app's GARCH(1,1)-t core

### Takeaway
Katsiampa (2017) found a two-component AR-CGARCH the best in-sample fit for 2010–2016 daily Bitcoin (mean daily return 0.58 %, sd 6.2 %, positively skewed, leptokurtic); later studies favour asymmetric and non-Gaussian innovations with Student-t degrees of freedom around 3–5, so the app's GARCH(1,1)-t with η = 1/ν on [0, 0.4] (ν ≥ 2.5) and the ±22 innovation bound is usable but should expect ν near its heavy end, an at-bound warning, and a leverage term it does not have; Deribit's DVOL (30-day implied vol) is keyless from 2021-03-24 and Binance hourly klines give realised vol from 2017/2019.

### Cited Findings
- Katsiampa, "Volatility estimation for Bitcoin: A comparison of GARCH models", *Economics Letters* 158 (2017), pp. 3–6 — [SHU archive](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/16526/); [author manuscript PDF](http://shura.shu.ac.uk/16526/1/Katsiampa-VolatilityEstimationforBitcoin(AM).pdf). Data: CoinDesk Bitcoin Price Index daily closes, 2010–2016; "the daily average return is equal to 0.5778 % with a standard deviation of 0.0617", returns "positively skewed", excess kurtosis indicating "leptokurtic behaviour", Jarque–Bera rejects normality, ARCH(5) confirms ARCH effects; models compared: AR-GARCH, EGARCH, TGARCH, APARCH, CGARCH, ACGARCH; selection by AIC, BIC and Hannan–Quinn; result: "the AR-CGARCH model appears to be an appropriate tool", i.e. both a short-run and a long-run variance component matter — [author manuscript PDF](http://shura.shu.ac.uk/16526/1/Katsiampa-VolatilityEstimationforBitcoin(AM).pdf).
- Later comparison: Gyamerah, "Estimating the volatility of Bitcoin using GARCH models" (arXiv 1909.04903) compares sGARCH, iGARCH and tGARCH with several distributions and finds the threshold-GARCH with normal-inverse-Gaussian innovations (tGARCH-NIG) best, the NIG capturing "the fat tails and skewness" — [arXiv 1909.04903](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04903).

- **Pre-registered validation criterion:** the 3-week CMOM long-short earns a positive mean weekly return with Newey–West t > 2.0 over the full Binance sample net of costs, and the top-minus-bottom momentum quintile spread is positive in at least 60 % of calendar years; the size spread is *reported but not scored* (survivorship).
- **Expected edge and known decay:** LTW 2.7–4.1 %/week gross in 2014–2018 on a 1,000+-coin universe; on a 50-name survivor set with costs expect far less and possibly zero; LTW's 8-week+ sorts already fail; He et al. show costs cut Sharpe by ~70 %.
- **Complexity:** 3
- **Caveats:** point-in-time membership unavailable keyless; CoinGecko public tier ~30 calls/min and 365-day history; weekly rebalancing of 50 names on Upbit incurs 20 bp round trips.

### C2. BTC/ETH short-horizon time-series momentum and MA-ratio trend
- **Asset class:** crypto (BTC-USD, ETH-USD; KRW-BTC on Upbit)
- **Family:** momentum
- **Mechanism:** s_t^{(k)} = sign(ln P_t − ln P_{t−7k}) for k = 1..4 weeks; m_t^{(n)} = P_t / MA_n(P) − 1 for n ∈ {10, 20, 50, 100, 200}; position ∈ {0, 1} (long/flat, no shorting for a Korean spot account); composite = mean of the sign votes, matching the Signals page's six-component composite.
- **Sources (verified):** LT 2021 ([NBER w24877 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf): +3.16 % per 1-sd at 1 week, +0.33 % per 1-sd daily); Detzel et al. 2021 *Financial Management* ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/fima.12310): price-to-MA ratios forecast daily BTC in and out of sample); LTW: 8-week+ momentum insignificant ([NBER w25882 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf)).
- **User-material refs:** QUANTGEN Signals page and costed backtest (README "Signals"); 계량2 ch. 5 (trend elimination, §5.2) for the MA as a trend filter.
- **Data needs:** BTC-USD, ETH-USD daily — Yahoo via yfinance (`BTC-USD` from 2014-09-17, `ETH-USD` from 2017-11-09); KRW-BTC daily — `GET https://api.upbit.com/v1/candles/days?market=KRW-BTC&count=200&to=<ISO>` (from 2017-09-25, 200 per call); hourly for seasonality — Binance `/api/v3/klines?interval=1h&limit=1000` (from 2017-08-17).
- **Reachable via:** yfinance, upbit-public, binance-public.
- **Backtest design:** daily signal at 00:00 UTC, one-day hold with hysteresis (switch only when the composite crosses ±0.2) to limit turnover; cost 10 bp per switch on Upbit; benchmark buy-and-hold; universe BTC and ETH separately; lookback sweep 1 week to 12 months reported as a heat-map.
- **Pre-registered validation criterion:** out-of-sample (walk-forward, 1-year folds, parameters frozen from the prior fold) Sharpe of the long/flat composite ≥ buy-and-hold Sharpe with a stationary-bootstrap p < 0.05, and max drawdown at least 25 % smaller than buy-and-hold; the 1–4-week lookbacks must pass individually while the 6–12-month lookbacks are expected to fail (a pass there is flagged, not celebrated).
- **Expected edge and known decay:** weekly autocorrelation documented to 2018; He et al. and LTW indicate decay with liquidity and arbitrage; weekend return filters expected null (Baur et al.).
- **Complexity:** 2
- **Caveats:** long/flat only; hysteresis parameter is a degree of freedom to fix ex ante; Yahoo hourly bars limited to 730 days.

### C3. Kimchi-premium monitor (signal, not a trade)
- **Asset class:** crypto / FX (KRW)
- **Family:** structural (arbitrage-deviation signal)
- **Mechanism:** p_t = P^{Upbit}_{KRW-BTC,t} / (P^{Binance}_{BTCUSDT,t} · S^{USDKRW}_t) − 1 daily; z-score on a 1-year window; ADF and KPSS on p_t (계량2 §5.3.2, §5.3.5) with a structural-break variant (§5.3.4); AR(1) half-life ln 2 / −ln φ; Korean retail-flow proxy f_t = Upbit `candle_acc_trade_price` (KRW) / (Binance quote volume × S_t); predictive regression r^{BTC}_{t+1..t+7} = a + b·z_t + ε.
- **Sources (verified):** Makarov & Schoar 2020 JFE ([LSE PDF](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/100409/1/Cryptocurrency_Markets_JFE_final_v4.pdf)); Choi, Lehar & Stauffer ([SNB seminar PDF](https://www.snb.ch/dam/jcr:474400ba-255b-49a4-b2b8-5e3626719730/sem_2019_05_31_lehar.n.pdf); [SSRN 3189051](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3189051)); Travel Rule 2026 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/south-korea-travel-rule-threshold-crypto-transfers)).
- **User-material refs:** 계량2 ch. 5 (unit roots) and ch. 6 (cointegration of the KRW and USD log prices, single-equation §6.2); Shreve II ch. 9 (FX under the domestic measure — the premium is a violation of one-price, not of CIP); the app's FX page for the USD/KRW series.
- **Data needs:** Upbit KRW-BTC daily (2017-09-25 →, 200/call); Binance BTCUSDT daily (2017-08-17 →, 1,000/call); USD/KRW — FRED `https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=DEXKOUS` (1981 →, ~1-week lag) for history and Yahoo `KRW=X` (2003-12-01 →) for the live value; optional Upbit KRW-ETH and Binance ETHUSDT for an ETH premium.
- **Reachable via:** upbit-public, binance-public, FRED, yfinance.
- **Backtest design:** no position; the "backtest" is the predictive regression and the unit-root battery on 2017-09-25 → today, with sub-samples before/after Jan 2018 (real-name accounts) and after the 2026 Travel Rule decree; benchmark: none (a monitor).
- **Pre-registered validation criterion:** (i) ADF rejects a unit root in p_t at 5 % on the full sample and the AR(1) half-life is below 30 days; (ii) the coefficient b on z_t for the next-7-day BTC return is negative with Newey–West t < −2 (high premium = crowded Korean retail = weaker forward return, the Makarov–Schoar buying-pressure reading); if (ii) fails, the premium stays a descriptive tile and is never fed into the composite.
- **Expected edge and known decay:** none as a trade (FETA remittance ceiling, Travel Rule, 22 % tax from 2027); as a sentiment read, the premium spiked to 15–54 % in Dec 2017–Jan 2018 and reads −0.24 % today, so the informative episodes are rare.
- **Complexity:** 1
- **Caveats:** FRED fixing is NY noon, Upbit/Binance closes are 00:00 UTC — a few hours of FX drift; Upbit and Binance daily volumes are in different quote currencies; the 2018–2020 average premium (2.27 %) comes from a search snippet, not the paper page.

### C5. Bitcoin volatility: GARCH(1,1)-t core versus realised vol and DVOL, scored out of sample
- **Asset class:** crypto (BTC, ETH)
- **Family:** volatility
- **Mechanism:** the existing `core/quant/garch.py` fit (σ²_t = ω + α ε²_{t−1} + β σ²_{t−1}, t_ν innovations, η = 1/ν ∈ [0, 0.4], bounded draws at ±22), annualised with 365 not 252; RV_d = Σ_{h∈day} r²_h from hourly klines, ×365; implied σ^{DVOL}_t = DVOL/100; forecast losses: QLIKE(σ̂², RV) = RV/σ̂² − ln(RV/σ̂²) − 1; VaR_99 coverage test (Kupiec) on the 1-day GARCH-t quantile; variance-risk premium VRP_t = DVOL² − RV_{30d}.
- **Sources (verified):** Katsiampa 2017 *Economics Letters* 158:3–6 ([SHU](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/16526/)); Gyamerah ([arXiv 1909.04903](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04903)); Deribit DVOL ([Deribit Insights](https://insights.deribit.com/exchange-updates/dvol-deribit-implied-volatility-index/); [API docs](https://docs.deribit.com/api-reference/market-data/public-get_volatility_index_data)); V-Lab ν = 5.02 ([V-Lab](https://vlab.stern.nyu.edu/volatility/VOL.BTCX/B:CN-R.GAS-GARCH-T)).
- **User-material refs:** primer ch. 4–7 (esp. 6.3 "Reading ν", 6.4 LR test, 7.7 guardrails); `docs/ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md` A2 (bounded innovations, Z_BOUND 22 and the variance dropped at ν < 4), B1 (η parametrisation, LR 2.706), B2 (no SEs; "P-estimates of tails need decades"), B5 (never scored out of sample), B8 (no leverage term); STAT248 §21.2.3 (MLE asymptotics for SE(η)); 계량2 ch. 8 ARCH; Meucci P-world estimation.
- **Data needs:** daily closes — Yahoo `BTC-USD` (2014-09-17 →) or Binance `/api/v3/klines?interval=1d`; hourly — Binance `/api/v3/klines?symbol=BTCUSDT&interval=1h&limit=1000` (2017-08-17 →) or `/fapi/v1/klines?interval=1h&limit=1500` (2019-09-08 →); implied vol — `GET https://www.deribit.com/api/v2/public/get_volatility_index_data?currency=BTC&resolution=1D&start_timestamp=<ms>&end_timestamp=<ms>` (2021-03-24 →, 1,000 rows/call with `continuation`).
- **Reachable via:** yfinance, binance-public, other:deribit-public.
- **Backtest design:** rolling 730-day fit, refit weekly, one-day-ahead forecasts from 2019 → today; competitors: EWMA(λ = 0.94) and the 30-day RV carried forward; report QLIKE, Kupiec/Christoffersen tests, and the fraction of days with a "bound visible" note; no positions.
- **Pre-registered validation criterion:** GARCH(1,1)-t QLIKE ≤ EWMA QLIKE over the out-of-sample period (Diebold–Mariano p < 0.10) **and** 1-day 99 % VaR exceptions between 0.5 % and 1.5 % of days (Kupiec not rejected at 5 %); if the first fails the tile recommends the RV-carry-forward as the crypto default, and the leverage-term upgrade (B8) is scheduled.
- **Expected edge and known decay:** none as a trade; the model improvement is measurable; expect ν ≈ 3–5 and α + β near 0.95–0.99; Katsiampa's two-component result implies a one-component GARCH under-reacts after regime shifts.
- **Complexity:** 2 (the fit exists; the scoring loop is new)
- **Caveats:** 365-day annualisation must be switched on for crypto everywhere the app prints σ; DVOL history is only from 2021; the ±22 bound drops 4–5 % of variance at ν ≈ 3 and CVaR sits several standard errors low (ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW §1.3) — the note must show on the crypto tile.
