## Key question 1: Factor models (FF3, FF5, Carhart), Ken French daily data, rolling regressions with Newey–West errors, the HLZ hurdle

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- Model to implement (daily, excess returns): r_it − r_ft = α_i + β_i (R_mt − r_ft) + s_i SMB_t + h_i HML_t [+ m_i MOM_t] [+ r_i RMW_t + c_i CMA_t] + ε_it. Use `RF` from the same Ken French file so the risk-free convention matches the factors. For a KRX name regressed on US factors the exercise is a *global-factor exposure* read, not a home-market test; for a proper Korean test build SMB/HML/MOM from pykrx (`get_market_cap`, `get_market_fundamental` PBR) using the FF rules above (2×3 sorts, June rebalance, big = top 90 % of cap; monthly or daily).
- Rolling window: W = 252 sessions (one year) stepping 21; L for HAC = the textbook plug-in ⌊4(T/100)^{2/9}⌋ (≈ 5 at T = 252) — attributed to Newey–West 1994 in most textbooks; the exact rule was not read from the paper (UNVERIFIED wording). Annualise α̂ by ×252 (daily arithmetic α) and report t(α) = α̂ / SE_HAC(α̂); the loadings with HAC SEs; R²; and the incremental R² from adding MOM / RMW+CMA.
- What the app should display per name: (1) α_ann with t(α) and a "clears HLZ 3.0?" flag; (2) β_mkt, s, h, m, r, c with 95 % HAC bands; (3) R² and residual vol; (4) a rolling chart of β and α; (5) a tile converting the residual Sharpe to a t-stat, t ≈ SR_ann·√(T/252), the conversion already specified in the validation note. A single name's α over one year (T = 252) almost never clears t > 3 — that is the honest message, and the point of showing the hurdle.
- Redundancy check to build in: regress HML on the other four factors (FF 2015's finding that HML is redundant in their sample) and show its α — a cheap, instructive replication.

## Key question 2: Cross-sectional anomalies — momentum (12-1), momentum crashes, short-term reversal, betting-against-beta, quality-minus-junk, PEAD; Korean evidence; post-publication decay

### Takeaway
All seven anchor papers are verified. Momentum (Jegadeesh–Titman 1993) is "significant" over 3–12-month holds, is driven by months 12–7 rather than the recent past (Novy-Marx 2012), crashes in "panic states" after market declines when volatility is high (Daniel–Moskowitz 2016), and is absent in Japan (Fama–French 2012: "Except for Japan, there is return momentum everywhere"). Korea-specific momentum is weak in the cross-country evidence only indirectly (Chui–Titman–Wei 2010: momentum is higher in individualistic countries; Korea ranks low on Hofstede individualism), and no Korean-language paper could be verified this session — so the app must test it directly. Post-publication decay is large in the US (McLean–Pontiff 2016: returns 26 % lower out-of-sample, 58 % lower post-publication) but Jacobs–Müller 2020 find the US "is the only country with a reliable post-publication decline" among 39 markets.

### Inferences (formula-level mechanisms)
- **12-1 momentum.** Signal at month-end t: M_it = Π_{k=2}^{12}(1 + r_{i,t−k}) − 1 (months t−12 … t−2, skipping t−1 to avoid the Jegadeesh 1990 one-month reversal). Daily version: cumulative return over sessions [t−252, t−21]. Rank cross-sectionally; long top decile, short bottom decile (or long-only top decile vs benchmark for a retail investor who cannot short); rebalance monthly, hold one month (or overlapping 6-month tranches per Jegadeesh–Titman). Novy-Marx variant: use months t−12 … t−7 only.
- **Crash guard (Daniel–Moskowitz).** Panic state = trailing 24-month market excess return < 0 AND trailing 6-month realised market vol above its median (the paper's bear indicator × vol; exact windows are body, UNVERIFIED). Dynamic weight w_t ∝ μ̂_t / σ̂²_t (their mean-variance scaling) — in practice: halve or zero the momentum position in panic states. Pre-registered test: does the panic-state conditional mean of the WML return differ from the rest (Welch t) and does the scaled strategy raise Sharpe out of sample?
- **Short-term reversal.** Rank on prior 1-month (Jegadeesh) or 1-week (Lehmann) return; long losers, short winners. This is the anomaly most exposed to bid-ask bounce and to costs; at KRX cost levels (see KQ3) a weekly reversal is very unlikely to survive for retail — treat as a *signal* input (e.g., to time entries of other strategies), not a standalone.
- **BAB.** β̂_i from daily data (FP 2014 estimate vols on one year of daily returns and correlations on five years of three-day overlapping returns, then shrink β̂ = 0.6 β^TS + 0.4 — body, UNVERIFIED); rank stocks by β̂, weight low-beta leg and high-beta leg by rank, and lever each leg to β = 1: BAB_t = (r_L − r_f)/β_L − (r_H − r_f)/β_H. Retail can hold the long low-beta leg unlevered; the point of the factor (leverage of the low-beta leg) is exactly what a Korean retail account cannot do cheaply.
- **QMJ.** Per-stock quality z-score = z(profitability) + z(growth) + z(safety) (each itself an average of z-scored accounting ratios: GPOA, ROE, ROA, CFOA, accruals; five-year growth of the same; low beta, low leverage, low bankruptcy risk, low earnings volatility — component list is body, UNVERIFIED); QMJ = 1/2(Small Quality + Big Quality) − 1/2(Small Junk + Big Junk) on 2×3 size×quality sorts. ### Inferences (formula-level mechanisms)
- **Distance method (GGR).** Formation window F = 252 sessions: normalised price P̃_it = Π_{s≤t}(1 + r_is) (dividends reinvested, start = 1). Distance D_ij = Σ_{t∈F}(P̃_it − P̃_jt)². Pick the N smallest-distance pairs from the universe (GGR: top 5 / top 20). Trading window = next 126 sessions: spread S_t = P̃_it − P̃_jt; open when |S_t| > 2 σ_F (σ_F = formation-window sd of S), long the low leg and short the high leg with equal won amounts; close when S crosses zero; force-close at the end of the window. Return convention: "committed capital" (divide by N pairs) vs "employed capital" (only open pairs) — report both. Costs: charge each leg's round trip.
- **OU residual model (Avellaneda–Lee).** Factor step: r_i = Σ_j β_ij F_j + ε_i with F_j = the first m eigenportfolios of the correlation matrix of a 252-day return panel (their m = 15, or the count explaining 55 % of variance — body, UNVERIFIED) or sector ETF returns. Cumulative residual X_t = Σ_{s≤t} ε_is. Model dX = κ(m − X)dt + σ dW. Estimate over a 60-session window by the AR(1) regression X_{n+1} = a + b X_n + ζ_{n+1}: κ = −ln(b)·252, m = a/(1 − b), σ_eq = √(Var(ζ)/(1 − b²)). Keep only stocks with κ > 252/30 (mean-reversion time under 30 sessions). Signal s_t = (X_t − m)/σ_eq. Rules: open long when s < −1.25; open short when s > +1.25; close long when s > −0.50; close short when s < +0.75 (asymmetric exits as in the paper; the assignment's "exit ±0.5" is the symmetric simplification). Positions are dollar-neutral and factor-hedged by −β_ij in the eigenportfolios/ETFs; their cost assumption is in the body (UNVERIFIED; commonly quoted as a few bp per trade).
- **Half-life.** From the AR(1) slope φ (= b above) on the spread or residual: HL = −ln 2 / ln φ sessions; equivalently ln 2 / κ with κ = −ln φ per session (×252 for annual κ). This is the same AR(1) autocovariance decay ρ(τ) = φ^τ in Kirchgässner §2.1 pp. 27 ff. Report HL with a bootstrap band; if φ ≥ 1 (unit root, ADF not rejected) HL is undefined and the pair is not tradable.
- **Cointegration route.** Step 1 (formation window only): y_t = a + b x_t + u_t by OLS (log prices), b = hedge ratio. Step 2: ADF on û_t with the Engle–Granger (MacKinnon) critical values, not the plain DF table (Kirchgässner §6.2.3 p. 215 / p. 219). Then the ECM Δy_t = α(y_{t−1} − a − b x_{t−1}) + Σ γ_k Δy_{t−k} + Σ δ_k Δx_{t−k} + e_t gives the adjustment speed α (Kirchgässner §6.3.1 p. 225). Multi-leg baskets (e.g. 005930/005935/000660): Johansen trace test with det_order 0, k_ar_diff chosen by AIC (Kirchgässner §6.3.2 pp. 228–238; Stata `vecrank` p. 17). Z-score of the spread z_t = (û_t − μ_F)/σ_F with μ_F, σ_F from the formation window.
- **Look-ahead traps to encode as tests.** (1) Hedge ratio b, μ_F, σ_F must be estimated on data ≤ the trade date; estimating b over the trading window itself makes the spread stationary by construction and inflates the Sharpe. (2) Pair selection (distance or cointegration p-value) must also use only the formation window. (3) Rolling re-estimation is fine if the window ends at t. (4) Survivorship: build the universe from constituents as of the formation date (pykrx `get_index_portfolio_deposit_file` by date). The app's `run_backtest` already shifts positions by one session and charges `cost_bps` on position changes — [core/quant/backtest.py lines 107–119](file:///C:/Users/whydi/OneDrive/Desktop/QUANTGEN/core/quant/backtest.py); a pair backtest needs the same shift on *both* legs and the sell-tax charged on each sell leg separately.

## Key question 6: Dual-listing / ADR arbitrage and the Korean 우선주 discount — are they executable at daily frequency by a retail investor?

### Takeaway
Gagnon & Karolyi (JFE 2010) measure ADR-vs-home price deviations for 506 cross-listed stocks from 35 countries at "an economically small 4.9 basis points" on average, "but they are volatile and can reach large extremes", and tie deviations to holding costs "even after controlling for transactions costs and foreign investment restrictions". For a Korean retail investor the ADR/local conversion is unavailable, the two closes are ~9 hours apart, and the short leg is constrained, so the ADR premium is a *monitor* (and a signal for which venue to buy in), not an arbitrage. The 우선주 discount is a genuine same-issuer spread with a live cross-section from KIS (`/ranking/prefer-disparate-ratio` FHPST01770000), but no Korean academic evidence on its predictability could be verified this session; it is executable long-only (tilt toward the preferred when its discount is historically wide) and as a pair only where 개인대주 covers the common.

### Cited Findings
- Gagnon & Karolyi, "Multi-market trading and arbitrage", *JFE* 97(1), 2010, 53–80. Abstract: "comparing the intraday prices and quotes of American Depositary Receipts (ADRs) and other types of cross-listed shares in U.S. markets with synchronous prices of their home-market shares on a currency-adjusted basis for a sample of 506 U.S. cross-listed stocks from 35 different countries. Deviations from price parity average an economically small 4.9 basis points, but they are volatile and can reach large extremes. Price parity deviations and their daily changes are positively related to proxies for holding costs that can impede arbitrage, even after controlling for transactions costs and foreign investment restrictions" — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v97y2010i1p53-80.html).
- KIS ranking endpoint `우선주 괴리율 상위` `/uapi/domestic-stock/v1/ranking/prefer-disparate-ratio`, tr_id FHPST01770000 (snapshot list) — [kis_api_audit.md, ranking table](file:///C:/Users/whydi/OneDrive/Desktop/QUANTGEN/research_notes/QUANTGEN%20asset%20coverage%20expansion/kis_api_audit.md).

### Inferences
- **ADR premium.** prem_t = (P_ADR,t × FX_KRW/USD,t) / (ratio × P_local,t) − 1, where ratio = local shares per ADR (e.g., Korean bank and telecom ADRs on NYSE — ticker list not verified). Using the US close (KST early morning) against the *previous* KRX close builds in a 9-hour stale-price bias; the cleaner daily quantity is the US close against the *next* KRX open, i.e., the ADR "predicts" the KRX open. A tradable retail use: if the ADR closed well above parity, the KRX open will likely gap up — a one-sided, long-only timing input, not a two-legged arbitrage. Test: regress the KRX open-to-previous-close return on lagged prem_t; report Newey–West t and the hit rate; expect the coefficient to be near one at the open and the residual premium to vanish within the session.
- **우선주 discount.** disc_t = 1 − P_pref,t / P_common,t. Mechanism: identical cash-flow participation plus a small extra dividend for the preferred (Samsung's 우선주 terms — UNVERIFIED), no vote, lower liquidity; the discount is a liquidity-and-control premium that mean-reverts slowly. Long-only implementation: within a Samsung (or Hyundai, LG, etc.) allocation, hold the preferred when disc_t is above its rolling 3-year 80th percentile and the common when below the 20th — no short leg. Pair implementation (short common / long preferred when wide) needs 개인대주 in the common and pays the 0.20 % sell tax on every unwind. Pre-registered test: ADF on ln(P_common/P_pref) over rolling 3-year windows must reject a unit root in ≥ 60 % of windows (Kirchgässner §5.3.2 / §6.2.1), half-life < 120 sessions, and the long-only switch must beat a 50/50 common/preferred hold on Sharpe net of costs over two halves.
- **Dual-listed Chinese A/H, HK/US or JP/US** pairs are reachable via KIS overseas endpoints (`EXCD` for HKS/SHS/TSE — codes not captured in the audit's grep; confirm) but face the same non-convertibility for retail; treat as monitors.
