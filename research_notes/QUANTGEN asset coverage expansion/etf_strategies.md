## KQ1. NAV premium/discount and creation–redemption arbitrage: what Petajisto (2017) and Ben-David, Franzoni & Moussawi (2018) find; how premium is computed from the KIS ETF endpoints; what premium-reversal strategy is testable daily and its Korean evidence

### Takeaway
ETF premiums in the US are typically inside a ±200 bp band (about 100 bp after correcting for stale NAVs) and mean-revert at roughly 10 bp/day; the paper's long-discount/short-premium strategy earns a Carhart alpha of about 11%/yr before costs but the profits come from international and illiquid-underlying funds, not diversified US equity ETFs. Korean evidence on KOSPI200 ETFs shows a persistent small *discount* (KODEX 200 mean price error −0.237%) with absolute 괴리율 around 1% that is shrinking and is not autocorrelated beyond 1–3 days, so a daily premium-reversal rule on KOSPI200 ETFs has very little to harvest after ~5 bp round-trip costs; the testable version is a cross-sectional one on the less liquid KRX ETFs (overseas-index, bond, commodity) where NAVs are stale.

### Inferences
- Premium computation for the app: premium_t = (Close_t − NAV_t)/NAV_t from either KIS `nav-comparison-daily-trend` (FHPST02440200, dated range) or pykrx `get_etf_price_deviation` (already reports 괴리율 in percent). A same-day close-vs-NAV comparison on a KRX domestic-equity ETF is not stale (NAV is struck on the same KRX close), so Petajisto's peer-group correction is only needed for overseas-index KRX ETFs (e.g. 379800), whose NAV is struck on a US close that precedes the KRX close by several hours, and for bond/commodity ETFs.
- Petajisto's own decomposition says the diversified US-equity slice of his strategy earns nothing; the Korean KOSPI200 evidence (|PE| ≈ 1% but no autocorrelation beyond a day, and shrinking over time) points the same way. A daily "buy when 괴리율 < −x, sell when it reverts" rule on KODEX 200 / TIGER 200 has expected gross edge on the order of the 괴리율 itself (tens of bp at most, usually single-digit bp today per the FunETF snapshots) against ≈ 4–5 bp round-trip costs (see KQ8), so it should be tested but is expected to fail the gate on the two flagship funds.
- The testable daily strategy is cross-sectional: each day rank all KRX ETFs within a peer group (e.g. all S&P 500 trackers, all KTB trackers) by premium relative to the group mean; long the bottom decile, short (or simply underweight/avoid) the top decile; hold until the relative premium crosses zero or for a fixed 5 days. The app cannot short KRX ETFs (see KQ2 gaps), so the implementable form is long-only "buy the cheapest sibling" when the operator wants the exposure anyway — an execution-cost saver rather than an alpha.
- Ben-David et al. is a caution, not a strategy: ETF-driven arbitrage adds transitory volatility to the constituents, which supports using ETF-level (not constituent-level) signals and warns that intraday reversal in constituents is not reachable at daily frequency.

## KQ2. Leveraged and inverse ETF decay: Cheng & Madhavan (2009), Avellaneda & Zhang (2010); the exact realised-variance formula; estimating expected decay from GARCH forecasts; whether short-leveraged-pairs strategies survive borrowing costs in Korea

### Takeaway
Avellaneda & Zhang give the exact identity L_t/L_0 = (S_t/S_0)^β · exp{((1−β)r − f)t + β∫λ ds + ((β − β²)/2)∫σ² ds}: a ±2x fund loses 1× (long) or 3× (short) the integrated variance of the underlying, before fees and funding. With the app's GARCH term structure the expected drag is (β² − β)/2 · Σ E[σ²_{T+i}], which is a forecastable number in bp per horizon. Korean retail cannot straightforwardly short KODEX 레버리지 (개인대주 was launched for KOSPI200/KOSDAQ150 constituents; ETF eligibility is UNVERIFIED), so the implementable expressions are (i) holding both an inverse and a leveraged fund as a variance-short only when a realised-vol forecast says so, and (ii) using the decay formula to rank the cheaper way to hold a given exposure.

## KQ3. Same-index ETF pairs and tracking error (KODEX 200 vs TIGER 200 vs KOSPI200 futures vs the index): cointegration set-up, expected half-life, tradeability after ~1.5 bp commission and spread

## KQ6. Volatility-managed portfolios (Moreira & Muir 2017) and risk parity across ETF sleeves; what the critiques found (Cederburg et al. 2020)

### Takeaway
Scaling exposure by c/RV²_{t−1} (previous month's realised variance from daily returns) raises the market factor's alpha by 4.9%/yr and its Sharpe by ~25% over 1926–2015 and survives realistic costs (Moreira & Muir), but Cederburg et al. show that in real time, across 103 strategies, the managed versions "do not systematically outperform" and out-of-sample versions "generally earn lower certainty equivalent returns and Sharpe ratios"; the implementable, defensible form for QUANTGEN is a *capped* vol target (no leverage) on each ETF sleeve, with risk parity (Asness–Frazzini–Pedersen) as the cross-sleeve weighting the app already computes.

## KQ7. Bond ETF carry and duration timing; gold ETF and real-rate models

### Takeaway

### C2. Leveraged/inverse ETF decay forecast and cheapest-exposure selector
- **name**: LETF variance-drag model (Avellaneda–Zhang with GARCH forecast)
- **asset class**: KRX leveraged/inverse ETFs (122630, 114800, 252670) and their US analogues
- **family**: structural
- **mechanism**: expected log drag over N sessions D_N = ((β² − β)/2)·Σ_{i=1}^{N} σ̂²_{T+i} + ((β−1)r + f)·N/252 where σ̂² is the app's GARCH(1,1)-t variance term structure (eq. (10) of Avellaneda–Zhang with ∫σ² replaced by its conditional expectation); for β = 2 the variance term is −Σσ̂², for β = −2 it is −3Σσ̂². Output: expected decay in bp for 1m/3m/12m, the break-even realised vol at which the LETF underperforms β× the index by more than the futures alternative, and a "hold via LETF / hold via futures / do not hold" recommendation. Position: none by itself; it gates C3/C5 (no leveraged sleeve when D_N exceeds the expected premium × (β−1)).
- **sources**: [Avellaneda–Zhang SIAM 2010](https://math.nyu.edu/inmemoriam/avellaneda/SIAMLETFS.pdf); [Cheng–Madhavan SSRN 1539120](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1539120); KODEX 레버리지 fee 0.64% — [FunETF](https://www.funetf.co.kr/product/etf/view/KR7122630007)
- **user-material refs**: Shreve II §4.4.2–4.4.3 (Itô–Doeblin, GBM example; printed pages UNVERIFIED); primer §1.2 (p. 6), §5 GARCH(1,1) (p. 13), §5.4 conditional-vol path (p. 15); user's essay §6.8–6.9 (pp. 16–17)
- **data needs**: daily closes of the LETF and of its underlying ETF/index (since 2010 for 122630), fee f, a short rate r
- **reachable via**: KIS-domestic-stock, other:pykrx (기초지수 column), FRED (`IR3TIB01KRM156N` monthly proxy for r)
- **backtest design**: not a P&L strategy — a forecast test: each month-end, predict the next quarter's realised (ln L_T/L_0 − β ln S_T/S_0) from D_N and compare with the ex-post identity; cost n/a; benchmark: naive "decay = 0" and "decay = last quarter's"; universe: 122630, 252670, 114800 vs KODEX 200 / KOSPI200
- **pre-registered validation criterion**: out of sample 2021–2026, the GARCH-based forecast's mean absolute error of quarterly decay must be below both naive benchmarks' by ≥ 20% and the forecast–realised regression slope must lie in [0.7, 1.3] with R² ≥ 0.5 (Avellaneda–Zhang report "excellent agreement" ex post; the test is whether the *ex-ante* variance forecast keeps that)
- **expected edge and known decay**: no alpha claimed; the value is avoiding a known 1×/3× ∫σ² drag (≈ 4%/12% per year at 20% vol) and the 64 bp fee
- **implementation complexity**: 1
- **caveats**: KRX inverse funds are futures-based (roll, basis); borrow cost λ enters only for short funds; taxes differ (122630 reportedly 국내주식형, 252670/114800 기타 — UNVERIFIED)

### C3. Vol-targeted (capped) sleeve overlay
- **name**: Capped volatility target (Moreira–Muir, real-time form)
- **asset class**: any ETF sleeve (KODEX 200, 379800, SPY, QQQ, TLT, GLD, IBIT)
- **family**: volatility
- **mechanism**: w_t = min(1, σ*/σ̂_t), σ̂_t = GARCH one-step vol (or 22-day RV), σ* fixed ex ante at the sleeve's unconditional GARCH vol; rebalance every 21 sessions with a ±10% band; the remainder in cash at the FRED 3m bill (US) or a KRW money-market/KTB ETF. Return source: variance is more persistent than the risk premium, so cutting exposure after high-variance months raises the ratio E[r]/σ (Moreira–Muir); no leverage, so the claim is Sharpe/drawdown, not return.
- **sources**: [Moreira–Muir JF 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12513) / [NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22208/w22208.pdf); [Cederburg et al. JFE 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v138y2020i1p95-117.html)
- **user-material refs**: 재무경제학 Ch. 6 slides 6-20 to 6-22 (U = E(r) − ½Aσ², y* = (E(r) − r_f)/(Aσ²)); primer §4–§5; MTSA §8.1 ARCH (p. 285)
- **data needs**: daily closes (≥ 5 years), a cash rate
- **reachable via**: yfinance, KIS-domestic-stock, FRED
- **backtest design**: signal on close t, weight applied from t+1; 21-session steps; cost 5 bp (KRX) / 100 bp (US via KIS) per unit turnover; benchmark: the same sleeve buy-and-hold; universe: the seven sleeves above
- **pre-registered validation criterion**: out of sample 2016–2026 (fit σ* on ≤ 2015 data), Sharpe(managed) − Sharpe(buy-and-hold) ≥ +0.10 with a Ledoit–Wolf/bootstrap p ≤ 0.10 *and* max drawdown reduced by ≥ 25% *and* certainty-equivalent (A = 3) not lower than buy-and-hold net of costs — a direct comparison, per Cederburg, not a spanning alpha
- **expected edge and known decay**: market factor 1926–2015: alpha 4.9%/yr, +25% Sharpe; Cederburg: real-time versions of 103 strategies generally do not beat unmanaged — expect the gate to pass on drawdown, be marginal on Sharpe
- **implementation complexity**: 2
- **caveats**: cap at 1 removes the leveraged upside that produced much of the published alpha; σ* choice must be fixed before the OOS window
