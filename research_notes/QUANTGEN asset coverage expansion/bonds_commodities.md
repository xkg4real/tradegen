## KQ1. Curve carry and roll-down as a strategy; 2-5-10 butterfly with PCA-neutral weights; term-premium timing (Cochrane–Piazzesi, Ludvigson–Ng) and its out-of-sample failure (Thornton–Valente)

### Takeaway
Carry + roll-down is the one bond signal with a model-free definition (Koijen–Moskowitz–Pedersen–Vrugt: carry ≈ yield slope over funding − D_mod × roll-down yield change) and a diversified Sharpe near 1 across asset classes; the tent-shaped Cochrane–Piazzesi factor has in-sample R² up to 0.44 but Thornton–Valente show forward-rate predictors add no out-of-sample economic value over the expectations-hypothesis benchmark, so any term-premium timer in the app must be pre-registered against that benchmark, not against in-sample R².

### Cited Findings
- KMPV define carry as "the return if market conditions stay the same"; for bonds equation (15) gives carry ≈ (yield slope over the risk-free rate) − D_mod·(y^{T−1}_t − y^T_t), i.e. "the carry is the bond yield plus the 'roll down'", the roll-down being "minus the yield change times the modified duration" — [Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen & Vrugt, "Carry", NBER WP 19325 (2013), p.15](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19325/w19325.pdf); published as JFE 127(2), 197–225, 2018 — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v127y2018i2p197-225.html).
- KMPV compute bond carry from a synthetic one-month futures on a 10-year zero: F_t = (1+r^f_t)/(1+y^{10Y}_t)^{10}, spot S_t = 1/(1+y^{9Y11M}_t)^{9+11/12}, carry C_t = S_t/F_t − 1 (their eq. 16); "for countries with actual, valid bond futures data, the correlation between actual futures returns and our synthetic futures returns exceeds 0.95" — [NBER WP 19325, pp.15–16, 44](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19325/w19325.pdf).
- Cross-sectional carry portfolios earn "an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.7 on average" per asset class; the diversified carry portfolio across all asset classes earns "a Sharpe ratio of 1.1" — [NBER WP 19325, p.4](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19325/w19325.pdf). Carry strategies "are commonly exposed to global recession, liquidity, and volatility risks" — [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2298565).
- Cochrane & Piazzesi: "a single tent-shaped linear combination of forward rates, predicts excess returns on one- to five-year maturity bonds with R2 up to 0.44"; Fama–Bliss forward-spread regressions had "R2 about 18 percent"; the 5–1 yield spread alone gives "the 0.15 R2 value typical of Fama-Bliss or Campbell-Shiller regressions"; the single factor "explains over 99.5 percent of the variance of expected excess returns"; data are "the Fama-Bliss data (available from CRSP) of one- through five-year zero coupon bond prices", one-year horizon — [Cochrane & Piazzesi, "Bond Risk Premia", AER 95(1), 138–160, March 2005, pp.1–2, 5, 11](https://web.stanford.edu/~piazzesi/cp.pdf); [AEA page](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2F0002828053828581).
- CP report that "A standard monthly AR(1) yield VAR raised to the twelfth power misses most of the one-year bond return predictability" and that a moving average of forward rates raises R² to 0.44 with tent coefficients (lag-0 row) γ ≈ (−3.24, −2.14, 0.81, 3.00, 0.80, −2.08) on (const, y(1), f(2), f(3), f(4), f(5)) — [CP 2005, pp.2, 15 (Table, Panel A)](https://web.stanford.edu/~piazzesi/cp.pdf). They state the forecasts "survive in real-time estimates" and "generate trading rule profits" (Appendix) — [CP 2005, p.22](https://web.stanford.edu/~piazzesi/cp.pdf).
- Ludvigson & Ng: macro factors from a 132-series panel (1964:1–2003:12) forecast excess bond returns; adding CP to the factor regression raises adjusted R² "from 26% to 45%" for the 2-year bond; out of sample, "The models that include the five factors in addition to the CP factor have a mean-squared error that is anywhere from 81% to 94% of that of the benchmark that includes only CP and a constant", ENC-NEW significant "at the 1% or better level" — [Ludvigson & Ng, "Macro Factors in Bond Risk Premia", RFS 22(12), 5027–5067, 2009, pp.11, 15, 22–25](https://www.columbia.edu/~sn2294/pub/rfs09.pdf); [RFS page](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/22/12/5027/1577464).
- Thornton & Valente (RFS 25(10), 3141–3168, 2012): out-of-sample, "the information content of forward rates does not generate systematic economic value to investors"; forward-spread models "do not lead to higher out-of-sample Sharpe ratios or higher economic utility compared with investments based on a no-predictability expectations hypothesis model" — [RFS article page](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/25/10/3141/1573606) (abstract as summarised in search results; full text not fetched).
- Litterman & Scheinkman, "Common Factors Affecting Bond Returns", Journal of Fixed Income 1(1), 54–61, June 1991 — [JFI page](https://jfi.pm-research.com/content/1/1/54) (paywalled; the level/steepness/curvature decomposition is the paper's subject; the often-quoted "~98% of variance" figure could not be verified from the source in this session — UNVERIFIED).
- Diebold & Li find NS-AR(1) 12-month-ahead RMSE beats the random walk at every maturity (Table 6: 3m 0.739 vs 1.019; 1y 0.841 vs 1.197; 3y 0.918 vs 1.237; 5y 0.978 vs 1.191; 10y 0.981 vs 1.052 percentage points) and the Cochrane–Piazzesi forward-curve regression at 6 months (Table 5: NS-AR(1) 1y 0.669 vs CP 0.859; 10y 0.721 vs 0.905) — [Diebold & Li, NBER WP 10048 (Oct 2003), pp.29–31](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10048/w10048.pdf) (see KQ2).

## KQ2. Diebold–Li (2006) dynamic Nelson–Siegel: AR(1) factor forecasts and their evidence against the random walk; how it extends the app's static NS fit

### Takeaway
Diebold–Li fix λ = 0.0609 (loading on curvature maximised at 30 months), estimate (β₁, β₂, β₃) by OLS each month, forecast each factor with a univariate AR(1) fitted directly at horizon h, and beat the random walk decisively only at the 12-month horizon (RMSE 0.74–0.98 vs 1.02–1.24 percentage points) — at 1 month they are statistically indistinguishable from the random walk. The app's `fit_nelson_siegel` already produces the factor panel; the extension is a per-factor AR(1) at the horizon plus honest recursive evaluation.

### Cited Findings
- Model: y_t(τ) = β_{1t} + β_{2t}[(1−e^{−λτ})/(λτ)] + β_{3t}[(1−e^{−λτ})/(λτ) − e^{−λτ}], with λ fixed at 0.0609 "for all t"; "The value that maximizes the loading on the medium-term factor at exactly 30 months is λ = 0.0609"; OLS each month gives the factor time series — [Diebold & Li, NBER WP 10048, pp.7, 11, 36 (Figure 1 notes)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10048/w10048.pdf).
- Forecasting: "We model and forecast the Nelson-Siegel factors as univariate AR(1) processes"; β̂_{i,t+h/t} = ĉ_i + γ̂_i β̂_{it} "obtained by regressing β̂_{i,t+h} on an intercept and β̂_{it}" — "we directly regress factors at t+h on factors at t, which is a standard method of coaxing least squares into optimizing the relevant loss function, h-month-ahead RMSE" — [NBER WP 10048, p.12](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10048/w10048.pdf).
- Data and design: monthly US Treasury yields 1985:01–2000:12 at maturities 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 30, 36, 48, 60, 72, 84, 96, 108, 120 months; "We estimate all models recursively from 1985:1 to the time that the forecast is made, beginning in 1994:1 and extending through 2000:12" — [NBER WP 10048, pp.35, 37](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10048/w10048.pdf).
- Results: 1-month-ahead forecasts "although slightly better than the random walk and slope regression forecasts, are indeed only very slightly better" and Diebold–Mariano tests "indicate universal insignificance"; "our model's 12-month ahead forecasts outperform those of all competitors at all maturities, often by a wide margin"; "Seven of the ten Diebold-Mariano statistics in Table 7 indicate significant 12-month-ahead RMSE superiority of our forecasts at the five percent level" — [NBER WP 10048, p.16](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10048/w10048.pdf).
- 6-month RMSE (Table 5), NS-AR(1) vs random walk: 3m 0.517 vs 0.605; 1y 0.669 vs 0.779; 3y 0.750 vs 0.879; 5y 0.777 vs 0.861; 10y 0.721 vs 0.758. 12-month RMSE (Table 6): NS-AR(1) 0.739/0.841/0.918/0.978/0.981 vs RW 1.019/1.197/1.237/1.191/1.052 vs NS-VAR(1) 1.102/1.293/1.393/1.385/1.279 (3m/1y/3y/5y/10y) — [NBER WP 10048, pp.29, 31](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10048/w10048.pdf).
- The VAR(1) on factors is worse than the AR(1)s; the authors expect this because "unrestricted VARs tend to produce poor forecasts … due to the large number of included parameters" and the factors "display little cross-factor interaction" — [NBER WP 10048, p.13](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10048/w10048.pdf).
- Published citation: Journal of Econometrics 130(2), 337–364, Feb 2006 — [NBER page](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10048); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/econom/v130y2006i2p337-364.html).

User-material references:
- Berkeley Lecture 6 §1.1 (p.2–3): the AR(1) h-step forecast is φ^h x_t with stationarity iff |φ|<1 — DL's direct-h regression is the same object estimated at the horizon; Figure 5 (p.16) contrasts ARIMA and random-walk forecast fans, the benchmark DL are judged against.
- 계량2 textbook §2.1.1 First-order autoregressive processes p.27; §2.4 Forecasting p.78 (§2.4.1 minimum-MSE forecasts p.78; §2.4.3 evaluation of forecasts p.85 — the Diebold–Mariano comparison belongs here); §5.3 unit-root tests pp.165–182 (DL report ADF statistics for the three factors, their Table 3 notes p.35).

File: `…\STAT248(UC Berkeley)\Applied Time Series - Full Lecture Notes.pdf`.

### Inferences
- The app's `fit_nelson_siegel(tenors, yields)` fits τ freely per snapshot (`NSFit` has a `tau` field). DL's discipline is to *fix* λ so the factors are comparable across days; for QUANTGEN the extension is: (i) refit every day in `CurvePanel` with λ fixed (1/τ, τ in years: 0.0609 per month ≈ 0.7308 per year), (ii) store the (level, slope, curvature) panel, (iii) fit three AR(1)s directly at the chosen horizon on monthly-sampled factors, (iv) rebuild the forecast curve through `nelson_siegel`. ## KQ3. Bond time-series momentum and cross-asset carry (MOP 2012; KMPV 2018; AMP 2013); what a Korean retail investor can express with KODEX 국고채 ETFs versus KTB futures

### Takeaway
TSMOM is fully specified in MOP: sign of the past-12-month excess return, 1-month holding, position = 40%/σ̂_{t−1} with an EWMA daily-return variance (60-day centre of mass); it is positive for all 58 instruments 1985–2009 including every bond future. For a Korean retail account the cheapest expression of a duration view is KTB futures (₩100m face, ₩10,000 per 0.01 pt, deposit and education prerequisites) and the long-only alternative is KODEX 국고채 ETFs (114260 / 471230 / 439870).

### Cited Findings

## KQ5. US Treasury futures basis: CTD, conversion factors, the cash–futures basis trade and its leverage risk (Barth & Kahn); what daily data can measure

### Takeaway
The basis trade is long the deliverable Treasury financed in overnight repo, short the futures, earning the basis net of repo and margin carry; it grew to about half of hedge funds' Treasury positions and a quarter of dealer repo lending before March 2020 (Barth–Kahn). With daily keyless data the app can compute the *gross* basis and a futures-implied yield from ZN=F and the FRED par curve, but the CTD price, repo rate and hence net basis/implied repo need a dealer/CTD price feed.

## KQ6. Commodity futures: term-structure/backwardation and momentum (Gorton–Rouwenhorst; Erb–Harvey; Szymanowska et al.; Gorton–Hayashi–Rouwenhorst; Miffre–Rallis); roll yield from the front two contracts; Yahoo continuous contracts versus KIS history

### Takeaway
The basis (front vs second contract, annualised) is the single characteristic that prices the cross-section of commodity futures: high-basis (backwardated) portfolios beat low-basis ones by about 10% per year (GHR), basis sorts produce spot premia of 8–14% per year (Szymanowska et al.), and 12-month momentum earns ~13% per year long–short (GHR) with momentum portfolios "buying backwardated contracts" (Miffre–Rallis). Roll yield requires two dated contracts; Yahoo's `=F` symbols give only one spliced front-month series whose adjustment method is undocumented, so KIS `daily-ccnl` on two dated contracts (or the expiry roll dates) is required for any term-structure signal.

### Cited Findings
- Gorton & Rouwenhorst (FAJ 62(2), 47–68, 2006): equally-weighted index of commodity futures, July 1959–December 2004; "Fully-collateralized commodity futures have historically offered the same return and Sharpe ratio as equities", negatively correlated with equity and bond returns and "positively correlated with inflation, unexpected inflation, and changes in expected inflation" — [NBER w10595](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10595); [CFA Institute FAJ page](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2006/facts-and-fantasies-about-commodity-futures).
- Erb & Harvey (FAJ 62(2), 69–97, 2006): excess return = spot return + roll return; "the term structure of futures prices drives the roll return"; since 1982 heating oil's 5.5% p.a. excess return split into "spot return … about 0.9% and the roll return … about 4.6%", gold's −5.7% into −0.8% spot and −4.8% roll "because the gold futures market has always been in contango"; worked example: with July-2005 oil at $36.65 and July-2004 at $40.95, holding the deferred contract one year with an unchanged curve yields 11.7% ($40.95/$36.65 − 1) — [Erb & Harvey, unabridged working paper (Jan 6, 2006), pp.22–23](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Working_Papers/W77_The_tactical_and.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=903770). Equally-weighted rebalanced portfolios of zero-premium assets earned a "diversification return" (simulation: 3.84% p.a. compound excess return, Sharpe 0.38 vs 0.00 unrebalanced) — [Erb & Harvey pp.6–7](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Working_Papers/W77_The_tactical_and.pdf).
- Gorton, Hayashi & Rouwenhorst (NBER WP 13249, July 2007; published Review of Finance 17(1), 35–105, 2013): 31 commodities 1969–2006 (working paper; 1971–2010 in the published version per the RoF abstract); "the convenience yield is a decreasing, non-linear relationship of inventories"; basis defined "as the annualized difference between the nearest to maturity futures price and the next futures price"; High-Basis portfolio outperformed the EW index "by 5.42% annualized (t = 3.98)", Low-Basis underperformed "by 4.82% (t = −3.44)", High-minus-Low "averaged 10.23% annualized (t = 3.73)"; Low-Inventory minus High-Inventory 8.06% p.a. ## KQ8. Interest-rate model connections: Shreve II (Vasicek/CIR examples; ch.10 term-structure models, HJM, forward measures), Meucci §3.2, and reconciling P-dynamics (`ratesim`) with the Q-curve (`curves`) for a bond horizon-P&L study

### Takeaway
Meucci's table puts Vasicek and CIR in the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck row as "the base case sell-side processes to price bonds", while the app calibrates them by OLS on the *historical* short rate — a P-estimate — and then prices bonds with the same parameters as if they were Q; the reconciliation is to keep κ, σ from P (and, for Hull–White, θ(t) from the curve so t = 0 prices match) and to carry an explicit market price of risk λ so that the horizon-P&L distribution is simulated under P but each path's revaluation curve is a Q-curve.

### Inferences
- Reconciliation recipe for a bond horizon-P&L study: (1) P-dynamics: κ_P, θ_P, σ from `calibrate` on the daily short rate (SOFR/KOFR), with an ADF check per 계량2 §5.3 and a warning when κ̂ hits the clamp; (2) Q-curve at t = 0: `bootstrap_par_curve` (market); (3) the link: Vasicek's Q-drift is κ(θ_Q − r) with θ_Q = θ_P − λσ/κ for a constant market price of risk λ (Shreve II §10.2.1's affine-yield derivation gives the ZCB in terms of the Q parameters), and λ is *identified* by forcing the model's Q-curve at t = 0 onto the bootstrapped curve (one scalar; or Hull–White's θ(t) does it exactly — the app already does this); (4) simulate r under P, and at the horizon h build each path's Q-curve with (κ, θ_Q(or θ(t)), σ) and reprice; (5) report the P-distribution of Q-prices, which is Meucci's VaR use of Monte Carlo. - The DL result (KQ2) is the empirical warning: affine models fitted for pricing forecast worse than a random walk; the P-forecast layer of QUANTGEN should be DNS/AR(1) or the random walk, and the affine models should stay on the pricing/scenario side.

- backtest design: recursive estimation from 1990, first forecast 2005, h = 6 and 12; evaluation is forecast RMSE, then a duration-tilt P&L with ZN=F at cost ½/32 per side; benchmark = random walk (no change) and the forward curve.
- pre-registered validation (DL's own): 12-month RMSE below the random walk at ≥ 4 of 5 maturities with Diebold–Mariano p < 0.05 at ≥ 3; no claim at h = 1. Secondary: tilt P&L Sharpe > 0.3 after costs over 2005–2026.
- expected edge/decay: DL's 12-month RMSE gains of 7–30% vs RW (Table 6); the P&L translation is untested in DL and expected to be small; decays in ZLB periods where β₂ is pinned.
- complexity: 2
- caveats: overlapping 12-month errors inflate t-stats (use DM with HAC); λ fixed at DL's monthly value must be converted for the app's yearly tenors; VAR(1) on factors is worse (DL p.13) — do not "upgrade" to it.

### C4. Term-premium timing (CP tent factor) — diagnostic with a pre-registered failure test
- name: Cochrane–Piazzesi return-forecasting factor (recursive) with EH benchmark
- asset class: US Treasuries
- family: factor
- mechanism: from FRED par yields bootstrap zeros (`bootstrap_par_curve`), build 1-year forward rates f(n), n = 2..5 and y(1); recursive OLS of the average 2–5-year 1-year excess return on (1, y(1), f(2)…f(5)) → γ̂_t f_t; position in ZF=F proportional to γ̂_t f_t/σ̂, capped; report the factor's percentile on the Macro page regardless of trading.
- sources: CP 2005 pp.1–2, 5, 11, 15, 22 (https://web.stanford.edu/~piazzesi/cp.pdf); Ludvigson & Ng 2009 pp.15, 22–25 (https://www.columbia.edu/~sn2294/pub/rfs09.pdf); Thornton & Valente 2012 (https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/25/10/3141/1573606).
- user-material refs: Meucci §3.1 p.4 (estimating the risk premium is "a challenging task"); 계량2 §2.4.3 p.85 (forecast evaluation).
- data needs: FRED DGS1…DGS5 (or Gürkaynak–Sack–Wright zeros — not wired), ≥ 25 years; monthly.
- reachable via: FRED
- backtest design: expanding window from 1990, first forecast 2005, monthly rebalance, ZF=F execution at $7.8125 per side (¼/32 — UNVERIFIED tick; pre-register 1 bp yield per side); benchmark = historical-mean excess return (EH) and 50/50 bond/cash.
- pre-registered validation (Thornton–Valente design): out-of-sample R² (Campbell–Thompson) > 0 with Clark–West p < 0.05 AND mean-variance utility gain for γ = 3 > 0 versus the EH benchmark after costs. The written prior is that it fails; the deliverable is the test, not the strategy.
- expected edge/decay: in-sample R² up to 0.44 (CP), out-of-sample economic value nil (Thornton–Valente); Ludvigson–Ng's macro factors cut MSE to 81–94% of the CP benchmark but need a 130-series panel the app does not have.
- complexity: 2
- caveats: overlapping annual returns (MA(12) errors → Newey–West 18 lags per Ludvigson–Ng p.13); measurement error in par-bootstrapped forwards (CP p.2) — use a 3-month moving average of forwards as CP do.

### C5. Time-series momentum on bond and commodity futures (MOP)
- name: TSMOM-12/1, vol-targeted
- asset class: ZT/ZF/ZN/ZB, GC/SI/CL/NG/ZC/ZS (and KTB 3/10 futures when wired)
- family: momentum
- mechanism: σ̂²_t = 261 Σ(1−δ)δ^i (r_{t−1−i} − r̄)², δ/(1−δ) = 60; signal s_t = sign(Σ_{k=1}^{252} r_{t−k}) on excess returns; position_t = s_t × (40%/σ̂_{t−1}) per instrument; hold 1 month (or daily re-evaluation with the same 12-month window); portfolio = equal-weighted across instruments (~12% vol in MOP).
- sources: MOP 2012 pp.1, 5–6, 9–11 (https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf); AQR original data page (https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Time-Series-Momentum-Original-Paper-Data).
- user-material refs: "From Radon–Nikodym to Fung–Hsieh" §6–7 (trend = lookback straddle − variance swap); Models Primer §1.3 p.7 (annualisation constant: 252 in the app vs 261 in MOP); 계량2 §8.2.2 p.294 (GARCH(1,1) vs EWMA).
- data needs: daily continuous futures ≥ 3 years for σ̂ warm-up plus 12 months for the signal (Yahoo `=F`, roll-adjustment UNVERIFIED — see KQ6 test), or KIS `daily-ccnl` on dated contracts (≤ 40 rows per call, HHDFC55020100) to build a known-adjustment series.
- reachable via: yfinance, KIS-overseas-futopt, KIS-futopt (KTB)
- backtest design: signal on close t, trade t+1; monthly rebalance; costs: Treasury futures ½/32–1/32 per side, commodities 1 tick + broker commission (per-contract KRW fee — pre-register 0.05% notional per side); benchmark = passive long, equal risk (MOP Fig. 3); universe = the 10 Yahoo symbols above.
- pre-registered validation: pooled sign regression β_{12} > 0 with t > 2 (clustered by month, MOP eq. 2) on the app's own data AND out-of-sample (2010–2026, after MOP's sample) TSMOM Sharpe > 0.4 gross, > 0.2 net; sign-consistency: ≥ 8 of 10 instruments with positive TSMOM mean.
- expected edge/decay: MOP factor Sharpe ≈ 1 gross 1985–2009, 1.1 in the 1966–1985 out-of-sample; widely reported to have weakened post-2009 (not verified here); largest gains in crises (Q4 2008).
- complexity: 2
- caveats: roll gaps in `=F` series would inject fake momentum; excess returns need the T-bill rate (FRED DTB3, wired as rf); KIS overseas symbols/exchange codes unconfirmed.

### C6. Cross-asset carry: Treasury-futures carry and commodity basis (KMPV)
- name: KMPV carry, bonds + commodities
- asset class: US Treasury futures (synthetic carry from the zero curve) and commodity futures (front/second basis)
- family: carry
- mechanism: bonds — C_t = S_t/F_t − 1 with F_t = (1+r^f)/(1+y^{10Y})^{10}, S_t = 1/(1+y^{9Y11M})^{9+11/12} from the bootstrapped zero curve (KMPV eq. 16; ≈ slope − D_mod×roll-down, eq. 15); commodities — C_t = (F1_t − F2_t)/F2_t × 12/months(F1→F2) (KMPV use the nearest two contracts); cross-sectional: long top third / short bottom third by carry, monthly; time-series: long if C_t > 0.
- sources: KMPV NBER WP 19325 pp.4, 13, 15–16, 44 (https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19325/w19325.pdf); GHR NBER 13249 pp.21, 50 (basis definition, 10.23% H−L) (https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w13249/w13249.pdf); Szymanowska et al. pp.1–4 (spot vs term premia) (https://www.tias.edu/docs/default-source/Kennisartikelen/an-anatomy-of-commodity-futures-risk-premia-szymanowska-and-de-roon-and-nijman-and-van-den-goorbergh-(2013).pdf?sfvrsn=6).
- user-material refs: Meucci §3.2 Fig. 3 p.5 (OU/ARMA for the basis); Berkeley L3 pp.14–18 (regression forecasts, TS cross-validation).
- data needs: FRED zeros via `bootstrap_par_curve` (keyless; 9y11m point needs interpolation the `YieldCurve` already does); two dated contracts per commodity from KIS `daily-ccnl` (or expiry calendars); ≥ 5 years.
- reachable via: FRED, KIS-overseas-futopt, yfinance (execution proxy only)
- backtest design: monthly rebalance; commodity cross-section of 6 (GC, SI, CL, NG, ZC, ZS) is too small for terciles — use rank weights w_i ∝ rank(C_i) − mean; costs as in C5; benchmark = equal-weight long.
- pre-registered validation: Fama–MacBeth slope of next-month return on carry > 0, t > 2, over ≥ 60 months on the app's data; commodity basis H−L mean > 0 after costs; bond time-series carry: hit rate > 55% when |C| > 1 bp/month.
- expected edge/decay: KMPV Sharpe 0.7 per class gross; commodity spot premia 5–14% p.a. on sorts (Szymanowska), but term premia (0.5–2%) are of opposite sign — rolling nearby isolates the spot premium; decays in contango regimes (gold "has always been in contango", Erb–Harvey p.23).
- complexity: 3
- caveats: with 6 commodities the cross-section is noise-dominated — report it as a time-series carry signal per commodity instead; Yahoo cannot supply F2.

### C7. Commodity term-structure/backwardation and momentum cross-section (Erb–Harvey; Miffre–Rallis)
- name: Roll-yield sort with 12-month momentum tie-break
- asset class: commodity futures (COMEX/NYMEX/CBOT)
- family: carry + momentum
- mechanism: RY_t = ln(F1/F2) × 365/(T2−T1); long the positive-RY (backwardated) half, short the negative-RY half; secondary filter: only long if past-12-month return > 0 (Miffre–Rallis: momentum buys backwardated contracts); monthly.
- sources: Erb & Harvey WP pp.3, 22–23 (https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Working_Papers/W77_The_tactical_and.pdf), FAJ 62(2) 69–97; Miffre & Rallis JBF 31(6) 1863–1886 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037842660700026X); GHR pp.2, 21, 23 (https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w13249/w13249.pdf); Gorton & Rouwenhorst FAJ 62(2) 47–68 (https://www.nber.org/papers/w10595).
- user-material refs: "Radon–Nikodym to Fung–Hsieh" §7 (momentum book); Meucci §3.5.
- data needs: as C6 (two dated contracts per commodity, expiry dates from KIS `search-contract-detail` HHDFC55200000 — output fields undocumented); ≥ 5 years.
- reachable via: KIS-overseas-futopt (required), yfinance (front only)
- backtest design: monthly; costs 1 tick + commission per side; benchmark = equal-weight long, rebalanced (Erb–Harvey's diversification return is a benchmark trap — report both rebalanced and unrebalanced EW).
- pre-registered validation: long–short mean > 0 with t > 2 over ≥ 60 months net of costs; sign of RY predicts next-month return with hit rate > 55%; the result must survive dropping energy (CL, NG), which dominates backwardation episodes.
- expected edge/decay: GHR H−L basis 10.23% p.a., momentum 13.36% p.a. (1969–2006, 31 commodities, gross); with 6 commodities expect a fraction; contango-heavy years (2010s) are the decay case.
- complexity: 3
- caveats: continuous-contract construction is the whole game — the roll-date return must come from the dated contracts, never from `=F`.

### C8. US Treasury cash–futures basis monitor (proxy gross basis and implied yield)
- name: Treasury basis monitor (not a trade)
- asset class: ZN=F (and ZT/ZF/ZB) vs FRED curve
- family: arbitrage (measurement)
- mechanism: proxy CTD = synthetic 6.5–10-year bond on the bootstrapped curve, CF = its price at 6% yield (CME definition); gross basis B_t = P_CTD − F_t × CF (32nds); implied futures yield = `yield_from_price(Bond(coupon_CTD, T_CTD), F_t × CF)`; carry bound = (coupon − SOFR) × days/360 using FRED SOFR; net-basis-bound = B_t − carry, labelled "includes delivery option + repo specialness (unobserved)".

- reachable via: yfinance, FRED, KIS-overseas-futopt
- backtest design: none as a trade; validation is convergence: proxy gross basis → carry at expiry within a pre-registered tolerance.
- pre-registered validation: |B_T − carry_T| < 2/32 at the last trading day for ≥ 80% of expiries in 2015–2026 (otherwise the proxy CTD is wrong — switch to a CTD candidate set by remaining maturity and the 6% rule: below 6% yields the CTD tends to the shortest-duration eligible issue).
- expected edge/decay: none for retail — the CFTC calls the return "small" and it is leveraged 20–50× by funds (Barth–Kahn); the value is the Macro-page read of funding stress (basis widening as in March 2020).
- complexity: 2
- caveats: the CTD's actual price and term repo are not in any keyless feed; state the proxy loudly.

### C10. P–Q reconciliation for the bond horizon-P&L study (model, not strategy)
- name: Market-price-of-risk-consistent Vasicek/CIR horizon P&L
- asset class: any bond/swap priced by `ratesim`
- family: structural
- mechanism: κ, σ, θ_P from `calibrate` (OLS on Δr); λ chosen so the model's t = 0 Q-curve matches `bootstrap_par_curve` in least squares (Vasicek: θ_Q = θ_P − λσ/κ; CIR: κ_Q = κ + λσ… per the affine forms in Shreve II §10.2); simulate r under P (`simulate`), reprice at h with Q parameters, report the P-distribution of Q-prices; Hull–White already achieves the t = 0 match through θ(t).
- sources: Meucci §3.1–3.3 pp.4–6; Diebold–Li pp.16–17 on affine models' forecasting failure (https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10048/w10048.pdf); app code `core/ficc/ratesim.py` (local).
- user-material refs: Shreve II §4.4.3 Examples (Vasicek/CIR), §10.2.1–10.2.2 (affine-yield), §10.3 (HJM), §9.4 (forward measures); 계량2 §2.1.1 p.27 (exact AR(1) discretisation), §5.3 p.165 (near-unit-root κ); STAT248 §7.3 p.31; Models Primer §1.4 p.7.
- data needs: SOFR/KOFR daily (FRED SOFR wired; KOFR not), FRED par curve; none new.
- reachable via: FRED, other:KOFR
- backtest design: not a strategy; a study: for each month 2015–2026, simulate the 1-year horizon distribution of a 10-year par bond under (i) λ = 0 (current app) and (ii) λ-matched; compare realised 1-year P&L against the two predictive distributions.
- pre-registered validation: PIT (probability-integral-transform) histogram of realised P&L under the λ-matched model passes a Kolmogorov–Smirnov uniformity test at 5% while λ = 0 fails, or the study reports that neither does (which is itself the DL finding).
- expected edge/decay: none; it removes a silent inconsistency (Vasicek/CIR paths priced at P-parameters).
- complexity: 3
- caveats: κ̂ near the 0.01 clamp makes θ_Q ill-determined; a two-factor model (Shreve II §10.2.1) is the next rung if the one-factor PIT fails.
