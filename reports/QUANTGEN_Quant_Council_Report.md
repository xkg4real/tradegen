# Simulated Quants Rebuild QUANTGEN's Gate, Not Its Macro

QUANTGEN's costliest errors sat in the instruments that test its macro rules, not in the rules' economics, so the council's answer is a rebuilt gate and data layer rather than new signals. QUANTGEN's owner asked for a third, competitive model of review: seven simulated quantitative-finance archetypes audited the four earlier artifacts (the macro integration SPEC v1.2, its backlog, the research report and the visual page), filed **180 audit items, 26 of them FALSE-TRUEs and 20 FALSE-ERRORs**, and proposed 60 models; the simulated professors and executives returned 472 verdicts on that work, two independent data labs re-ran the load-bearing numbers, and three chairs and a secretary ruled on 361 items. Nothing failed to replicate, and only **3 of the 472 verdicts overturned a QF finding**, all of them in one statistics paper. The decisive findings were mechanical: every artifact's "2 Oct close" was an intraday print (about 10:30 KST, in a file written at 10:56 KST; the official close was **7,003.74**), the staleness defaults would have halted the engine on every decision day, the literal drawdown ladder never re-arms, a single-path drawdown test rejects a costless vol target roughly 55–70% of the time, the published power arithmetic was 2–2.5× too optimistic, and the deflated-Sharpe trial count was undefined. One verdict moved materially. The Korea 20-day vol target, parked as INSUFFICIENT DATA on an 11-year file, is an **indicative but fragile PASS** on 1997–2026, with a maximum drawdown 12.7 percentage points (pp) shallower than a static book of the same average exposure, and it becomes BUILD-AS-INSURANCE if a formal run on KRX official closes passes, although the adversarial review of SPEC v2.0 now expects that run to return INSUFFICIENT DATA once the trials already run on Korean data are counted; the US vol target's REJECT is now recorded as a fact about trading it in single US shares at 50 bp, and the USD/KRW and US-rates legs are settled REJECTs. QF was itself wrong twice on consequential points: it used the wrong formula for the effective trial count, and it counted two in-sample markets as a jump-model holdout. SPEC v2.0 therefore adds, changes, demotes or retires **34 modules (16 at P0)** across milestones M0–M10, freezes the whole gate package blind before any card runs, and keeps the principles intact: macro sizes risk and never sets direction, nothing exceeds 1.0×, and nothing places an order. Four rounds of adversarial review then tested SPEC v2.0 for buildability without reversing a council decision.

### Korea's vol target was parked on an eleven-year file

Five of the seven QF papers attacked one verdict. The SPEC graded the Korea vol target (R-06 KR: SMA20 volatility, 20% target, cap 1.0, rebalanced daily) INSUFFICIENT DATA because "eleven years of KOSPI cannot yet confirm it costs no Sharpe, and July 2026 supplies 59% of its benefit", and routed it to twelve forward months. The verdict was honest for its data, a Yahoo file starting in 2015 (`QF4` §4). On a file from December 1996 the same pre-registered card passed every protective check at the ETF and futures books: on April 1997 to October 2026 at 3 bp its maximum drawdown was **12.7 pp shallower** than S, its exposure on the worst 5% of days was 0.68 of its average, and non-inferiority gave p_ni 0.051 (`QF4-A1`; local: `QF4_korea_vt_long.csv`).

The cross-examination upheld the finding and then exposed its fragility. DL-A's definitive cell, fixed before the run, reproduced the PASS (p_ni 0.052, ΔSR +0.026), but under the SPEC's own episode table July 2026 carries **0.493 of the benefit against a 0.50 limit**, neither 1997–2015 nor 2016–26 confirms it alone, and 2016–25 without 2026 is a REJECT with a tail ratio of 0.95 (`QX_DLA` §2.2). EX2 found that the pre-ETF regime of 1997–2002 alone is also a REJECT (ΔSR −0.20), while the ETF era from October 2002 to December 2025 passes without 2026 (p_ni 0.040, ΔMDD +11.9 pp) (`QX_EX2` §1). PX2 showed that moving from raw returns with idle cash at zero to excess returns with cash at the Korean call rate cuts ΔSR from +0.025 to +0.006, lifts p_ni from 0.051 to 0.078 and lowers the share of 33 start dates that pass from 97% to 70%. It also found the card's mean-timing return is **−1.84% a year** against a variance ratio of 0.59, the signature of insurance that is short the post-crash rebound premium rather than of a timing bet (`QX_PX2` §3(4)). The protection itself never changes sign: 71 of 72 monthly starts from 1997 to 2003 pass, ΔMDD ranges from +3.7 to +12.7 pp mostly according to whether the sample contains the June-1997 peak, and no start in DL-A's or EX1's sweep is a REJECT (`QX_DLA` §2.1; `QX_EX1` §4.2). The old route was the weakest part of the verdict: twelve more months add 3–10% of the information already in the history and lift PC-3's power at a true ΔSR of zero only from 0.305 to 0.317 (`QX_PX1` §3.2).

### The US REJECT is a fact about single US shares at 50 bp

The SPEC rejected the US 15% vol target because PC-1 failed: drawdown improved by only 0.2 pp. Four papers called the label a FALSE-ERROR. The rule had been charged 50 bp per side for US shares bought through KIS, although a market-gross multiplier is a beta adjustment that the SPEC's own futures rate (5 bp) can execute, and the vol-targeting paper the SPEC cites ran it on futures at 1.0 bp for equities ([Harvey et al. 2018](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). On the identical 1990–2026 sample a futures line passes PC-1 to PC-6 (ΔMDD +3.6 pp, p_ni 0.002), and expected-shortfall metrics show protection with probability 0.99–1.00 (`QF1-A1`; `QF3-A4`). The labs accepted the label but not the optimism. The futures PASS clears PC-1 by only 0.6 pp, passes at only 11 of 44 quarterly start dates (every start from late 1994 to mid-2000 is a REJECT) and shows +0.3 pp on 1990–2007 (`QX_DLA` §3); on 1995–2026 it flips with the estimator, SMA20 giving +2.5 pp (a REJECT) while GARCH, log-HAR and a combined forecast pass (`QX_DLB` T1h). EX2 added the constraint a one-person desk cannot argue away: one Micro E-mini contract is about ₩52m, so a ₩0.1bn sleeve tracks the target with 7.5% a year of tracking error against 0.7% at ₩1bn (`QX_EX2` N3). QF withdrew "artefact" as a reason to build.

### Three mechanical defects would have broken a literal build

The intraday print is the cleanest FALSE-TRUE. SPEC §11, the golden fixtures, the report and the page all quoted KOSPI 6,943.66 as the 2 October close; the file was written at 10:56:46 KST, mid-session, and the official close was **7,003.74** ([SBS](https://news.sbs.co.kr/english/article.do?news_id=N1008780949); [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026100205346)). The numbers it moved were small (a drawdown from the 9,114.55 peak of −23.2% rather than −23.8%; a next vol-target weight of 0.694 rather than 0.691), but the mechanism was not: the SPEC's own lag audit, `available_at_utc ≤ decision_utc`, would have accepted the print, because nothing in the schema separated a provisional value from an official close (`QF2-A2`). A data patch replaced 8 closes and dropped 8 provisional and 39 partial-month rows, and DL-A recomputed 24 dependent values; follow-ups on 3 Oct dropped 3 duplicate-date rows and 2 more provisional rows (the 2-Oct CL=F and GC=F bars in `yahoo_long`), for 60 logged actions (local: `QF0_data_patch_20261002.csv`; `QX_DLA` §7). Two further defects were Critical. Under the §4.1 defaults every input from the Fed's weekly H.10 release, posted on Mondays at 4:15 pm ([Fed H.10](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h10/)), is stale at **100% of decisions** and strict-H.15 yields at 99.5%; because an input stale for more than one decision moves the engine to HALTED, a literal build halts every day, whereas a schedule-aware rule flags 0.3–0.9% of decisions, all on dates the source legitimately skipped (`QF2-A3`; `QX_DLA` §6). And the drawdown ladder's −15% hard stop defined no reference after re-arm. NAV is flat while the book is halted, so it re-halts at every re-arm and sits in HALTED from 1997 at 1.3% average exposure through 714 cycles on KOSPI; read literally, the same rule also made the −8% and −12% steps cumulative (0.25×) (`QF4-A5`, `A6`; `QX_DLA` §5).

### The power arithmetic understated the problem by 2–2.5×

The artifacts justified the protective class with "SE(ΔSR) ≈ 0.034 for the US and 0.052 for KOSPI; a +0.05 effect needs 33–44 years". Those figures assume an overlay about 0.98 correlated with its base, which holds for add-on legs. A market vol target correlates 0.89–0.92 with S, so the HAC standard errors are **0.061 (US) and 0.130 (KOSPI)** and detecting +0.05 takes roughly 146–330 years (`QF3-A3`; `QX_DLB` T5a). The executives, whose round-2 arithmetic the SPEC had compressed into that sentence, conceded; the conclusion that no backtest can judge a vol target on Sharpe holds a fortiori. The same audit found PC-1 unfit to gate. Single-path maximum drawdown has a bootstrap SD of 7.4–7.8 pp that does not shrink from 10 to 100 years, because expected maximum drawdown grows with the logarithm of sample length ([Magdon-Ismail et al. 2004](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/~magdon/ps/journal/drawdown_journal.pdf)), so PC-1 rejects a vol target with zero true Sharpe cost about 55–70% of the time in 11–36-year resampled histories (`QX_PX1` N2; `QX_DLB` T5b).

### Where QF itself was wrong

The most consequential retraction concerns step 3 of QUANTGEN's gate, the deflated Sharpe ratio. QF3 was right that the step was undefined for macro cards, since nothing fixed the trial count N, the variance or the benchmark, and right that no script in the programme computed a deflated Sharpe at all (`QF3-A1`, `A20`). It then rescued R-03 (momentum own-volatility scaling) by counting N as Bailey and López de Prado's "independent-trial" N̂ = M/(1 + (M − 1)ρ̄), which gave 1.1–1.8 trials and an easy pass. That is the Kish design-effect form; the paper's equation 9 interpolates linearly, N̂ = ρ̂ + (1 − ρ̂)M ([Bailey & López de Prado 2014](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)). On QF3's own 26-trial family the linear form gives 12.6 trials and puts R-03 at 0.946–0.947 (PX1; the council's re-check), just below the 0.95 bar. The Kish form also yields a negative expected maximum (−0.22) at N̂ 1.14, and it can be gamed by padding: 80 near-duplicates of R-03 lowered it from 1.81 to 1.20 and lifted the deflated add-on of R-04, the Daniel–Moskowitz (D&M) bear-state gate, from 0.951 to 0.986 (`QX_PX1` N1; `QX_EX1` §4.1). PX1 and EX1 overturned the formula, DL-B corrected it and QF conceded. Because two machine extractions of the PDF disagreed, the council made the gate independent of the reading: it gates on N_equiv, the iid trial count with the same simulated expected maximum (6.3 on QF3's family, 6.59 after padding), prints the linear N̂ as a conservative bound, and lists a check of the printed equation as a settling test.

The second retraction concerns regime models. QF7 correctly showed that the artifacts had filed persistence-penalised jump models with failed, unpenalised Markov switching, and that "KOSPI switches 30.8 times a year" came from a model first trained on two years of data (9.95 a year with long training) (`QF7-A1`, `A4`). It then claimed that a jump model with an "untuned" penalty λ = 50 protected the US and four held-out markets. PX1 traced λ = 50 to [Shu, Yu & Mulvey](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05272v3), who set it on the S&P 500, DAX and Nikkei over 1990–2023: two of the four "held-out" markets and most of the US sample. In the only clean US holdout, January 2024 to September 2026 at 50 bp, the jump model cut drawdown by 3.7 pp at a Sharpe cost of −0.128 against S while the executives' 200-day band cost −0.070, and the band also won on the full sample (ΔMDD +16.5 against +13.9 pp) (`QX_PX1` N5). DL-B added that QF7's Korean failure figure (−4.7 pp) came from QF7's own identification guard; the robust reasons to exclude Korea are no plateau in λ, flags that arrive at the trough and a PC-3 failure (`QX_DLB` T4d). QF kept one point. In the two genuinely clean markets, the FTSE and Hang Seng, the jump model beat the band (+11.6 and +7.5 pp against +6.8 and +5.1 pp), which is why a forward head-to-head went to the backlog rather than the archive.

The QF chair listed 21 concessions in all (`QC_Q_quant_chair.md` §2). Beyond these two, the ones that changed a rule were R-04's pooled "route to PASS" (its deflated add-on is 0.77 at the honest N, and its own pre-registered holdout passed in 1 of 3 regions); daily expected shortfall as a gate (a 5-day vol target at 50 bp passes it while deepening drawdown by 12.5 pp); QF5's "40.5% of years", which counted 17 tied years as losses; the KRX close bound (06:30:30Z, not 06:35Z, since the random end became unconditional in 2015); and "robust" as a description of the Korea pass.

## Disagreements were about definitions, not arithmetic

The three groups argued from different mandates, and each changed its mind on the record. The professors held the statistical line: pre-registration, counted multiplicity, no promotion on one path or one crisis, and a stated second-moment mechanism for every acting rule. The executives argued for what a one-person, read-only desk can run safely: fail-closed, simple, auditable and executable at the declared cost, with no "false comfort". QF argued for measurement, meaning the right instrument book, the right clock and the right statistic; as the audited party it answered 140 reviewer verdicts against it by conceding 108, defending 27 and revising 23, with a split answer counted in each class it names (`QC_Q_quant_chair.md` §0).

### Strengths and weaknesses: clean timing and honest costs, but no fills

QUANTGEN's strengths are real and cheap to keep. Its research code handles time correctly: every weight is built from data through t−1, and the one rule whose edge came from the future, a Korea SOX×KRW gate with a **look-ahead premium of 0.28 Sharpe**, had already been caught and now serves as the positive control in every card's timing test (`QF2-A16`, `A17`; R-34). Its cost table is conservative for the instruments it uses: KODEX 200 costs about **1.6 bp** a side against the table's 3 bp, and a Samsung-class stock about 20 bp against 35 bp, including the 0.20% sell tax in force from January 2026 ([Herald Economy](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10627001); `QF1` headline). Its 40% live haircut is calibrated rather than conventional: McLean and Pontiff's published version reports returns 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication, a further decay of about 43% beyond the out-of-sample level ([McLean & Pontiff](https://tevgeniou.github.io/EquityRiskFactors/bibliography/AcademicReviewFactor.pdf); `QF7-A12`). The weaknesses are structural: no fills, one price vendor, a notional too small for index futures and a single maintainer. The last argues for simplicity: the executives vetoed the jump-model engine in favour of an auditable band, citing the Federal Reserve's warning that "model risk increases with greater model complexity" ([SR 11-7](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm); `QX_EX1` §2(4)).

### Opportunities and threats: logging is cheap, while Korea and the gate stay fragile

The opportunities are mostly logging, which a read-only desk can start today. Snapshotting the KIS bid and ask at 15:19:59 KST beside the decision price, the closing-auction print and the simulated fill gives quote-snapshot TCA, the nearest substitute for the own-execution cost studies that AQR has published ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Trading-Costs); `QF1` §5). Storing first prints and vintages for revised FRED series, as R-13 now requires, lets later tests see the data as it was known. Publishing the script and seed of the app's GJR study would turn a cited but unverifiable VaR result into a reproducible one (`QF6-A25`). Logging KOFIA margin debt and leveraged-product AUM would give the Korea leverage state the data that QF1's failed volume proxy could not supply (`QF1-M5`).

The threats sit in Korea and in the gate. The Korea PASS is not expected to survive its formal run: once the selection factor is counted, the run is expected to return INSUFFICIENT DATA (see the adversarial-review section below), and the EWMA(0.94) line, v1.1's registered challenger, already returns INSUFFICIENT on PC-4 with a 0.51 episode share (`QX_DLA` §2). No vol target helps against gap shocks from calm states (2018-10, 2020-02, 2024-08), and no trend rule helps in fast crashes from an uptrend: every trend rule tested was fully long on 23 June 2026 and through July's −38.6% (`QF5-A9`). The largest threat is procedural. If statistics can still be chosen after results are seen, then, in the QF chair's words, "the protective class is decided by whoever chooses the statistic last" (`QC_Q_quant_chair.md` §3).

### Strategic implications: parity where cheap, refusal where the evidence says no

QUANTGEN should seek parity where the gap is measurement and the cost is code: data integrity and staleness, gate statistics, instrument books and the fill model. It should refuse where the evidence says no: a regime engine; a maintained ML classifier, since QF3's elastic-net and gradient-boosting models lost to a two-parameter EWMA rule even after PX1 gave them the feature QF3 had withheld (`QX_PX1` §0); a conformal label layer, since a constant volatility forecast passes the per-label coverage test (`QX_PX1` §3.5); and futures below the granularity floor. It should monitor what it cannot yet measure: own-fill costs, funding and crowding data, the pre-registered 2027–28 head-to-head, and the owner's decision on a US sleeve (D5).

## Limitations: simulated panels, one vendor and a fragile pass

The panels are simulated. Twenty fictional personas (seven QF archetypes, six professors and seven executives) argued from public sources in a structured debate, and their convergence partly reflects shared inputs, since the same local files and the same two data labs informed every group. None of it is a survey of practitioners or a view of any firm's internal practice.

The data come mostly from one vendor. The Korea results use Yahoo's ^KS11, a price index without dividends and with no second source before 2015 (`QX_DLA` §8). Yahoo omits the Saturday sessions KRX held before 12 December 1998, so 1997–98 Monday returns span two sessions and their variance is 2.30 times the Tuesday-to-Friday level, against 1.11–1.33 later (`QX_EX2` N1). The files also hold flat zero-volume rows, an 800-day hole in KODEX 200 (February 2007 to April 2009) and 778 negative adjusted closes for SK hynix up to December 2002, and the ^GSPC open equals the prior close on 94–98% of 1996–2005 days, so US range estimators fall back on Parkinson's high–low form (`QF1` §4.8; `QX_DLB` §1). The formal Korea run therefore needs KRX official closes, and the US futures run still needs the 1926 daily file that returned HTTP 403.

The Korea pass is fragile. July 2026 carries 0.493 of its PC-4 benefit under the SPEC's episodes; no sub-sample confirms it alone; 2016–25 and 1997–2002 are both REJECTs; only 2 of 20 rolling decades pass; and p_ni rises from 0.051 to 0.078 under excess returns. The objective episode rule that rescues PC-4 was adopted blind to every card but with its effect known, and under the conservative selection factor the formal run is now expected to return INSUFFICIENT DATA (previous section).

Much remains untested. Without KRX margin, leveraged-product AUM and stock-level data, the Korea leverage state and a calm-state reversal test net of 35 bp and the 0.20% tax remain open, and the Korea reversal prior, cut from 3 to 1, stays UNVERIFIED (`QX_PX2` §4; R-15). The KIS level-1 quote fields, the KRX post-close session's status after 14 September 2026, the app's GJR study on SPY and the printed form of equation 9 are all unverified. The gate-acceptance test is specified but has not run, and no v2 module has been built.

## Conclusion

The third panel changed the question from "which overlays work" to "which of our instruments can tell". The two consequential negative verdicts on the vol targets came from an eleven-year file and a retail commission rather than from evidence against the rules, and the most dangerous defects would have produced confident verdicts from broken measurement. The fix is therefore procedural before it is economic: adopt the whole gate package once, blind, with placebos and negative controls, and only then let any card speak. That ordering is the council's most transferable result, and it is the part of institutional practice a one-person desk can match without fills, funding data or scale.

The economics did not move. The rules that survive are second-moment rules: momentum's own volatility, Korea's own volatility and a slow trend read through the volatility state. The one material promotion is conditional and honest about its premium: Korea's vol target is insurance that pays in volatility-clustering crises and charges in calm decades and rebounds, and on current evidence its formal run is expected to return INSUFFICIENT DATA, so the card is not expected to build. The question that matters most for the desk is still local and still open: what led Korea's 2026 deleveraging. Answering it needs leverage and flow data that QUANTGEN does not yet log.
