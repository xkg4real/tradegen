# Korean law leaves $30 traders unlevered

*Research date: 2026-10-23. Scope: a South Korean national living in Korea (university student) with about USD 30 (KRW 42,000).*

## Bottom line

With KRW 42,000, a Korean resident can lawfully and practically reach only **unlevered products**: KRX cash shares and 1x ETFs (fractional from about KRW 1,000 at some brokers), US stocks and ETFs bought through a Korean broker, KRW spot crypto on FIU-registered exchanges (KRW 5,000 minimum order on Upbit), long-only US options with a premium of a few cents after a 1-hour course and 3 hours of mock trading, and the state lottery and Betman. Every regulated leveraged product sits behind a wall this account cannot climb. Domestic derivatives and leveraged ETPs need KRW 10–30 million deposits, and so, since **22 May 2026**, do US-listed leveraged ETPs for first-time buyers (the first-pass research said otherwise and was wrong). FX margin needs US$10,000 a lot, and CFDs need professional-investor status. Prediction markets are the clearest red line. Korea's content regulator blocked Polymarket on **18 August 2026**, and police have referred **18 Korean Polymarket users** to prosecutors under Criminal Act Art. 246. Kalshi does not list Korea as restricted, but its terms exclude anyone for whom trading is unlawful where they live. Any sports-outcome contract or offshore sportsbook carries **up to 5 years or KRW 50 million** under the National Sports Promotion Act, even when the operator is foreign. The only leverage within reach is grey: DEX perpetuals (Hyperliquid, up to **40x on BTC**) or unregistered offshore exchanges, funded through same-name wallet transfers. Those rules tighten between December 2026 and February 2027. Turning $30 into $30,000 in a month takes about **10 consecutive doublings**. Without a real edge, no strategy whose losses stop at the account balance can do that with probability above **0.1%**, and base rates put realistic odds at **0.01% or less**. The expected result is losing most of the stake.

## Risk areas

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Criminal gambling exposure from prediction-market trading (Criminal Act Art. 246; habitual gambling under Art. 246②) | High | Hard-block every prediction market for Korean residents. Key the block on KYC residence, not IP. |
| Sports-outcome contracts or offshore sportsbooks (≤5 yrs / ≤KRW 50M for the bettor) | Critical | Hard-block. Ship no sports odds, signals or routing, because Art. 26② reaches people who build systems or supply information for "similar acts". |
| Tool-builder or operator liability: opening a gambling space (Art. 247), sports-betting systems (Art. 26②), acting as an unregistered VASP by handling others' money (특금법), promoting unregistered exchanges | High | Single-user, own-funds software only. No pooled accounts, copy-trading, referral links or public signal channels. |
| Market manipulation under Virtual Asset User Protection Act Art. 10 (pump groups, wash trading, spoofing, misleading posts) | High | No coordinated-buy features, no self-matching across accounts, no layering logic, no auto-posting. |
| VPN or geo-evasion (ToS breach, frozen funds; can be read as knowing evasion) | Medium-High | Refuse to route through VPNs. Query platform geoblock endpoints. Never use Polymarket's unrestricted API from Korea. |
| Unregistered offshore exchanges: blocked withdrawals, asset freezes, funding route revoked without notice | Medium-High | Never designate them as primary venues. Warn on any transfer. Keep balances on registered VASPs. |
| FX-reporting breaches through foreign brokers or overseas deposits (fine ≤KRW 100M) | Medium | Route foreign securities only through Korean brokers. |
| Liquidation on 10–40x DEX perps; losses beyond the account on overseas futures | High (financial) | Hard-block products with recourse leverage. Cap leverage and position size on DEX perps, with explicit liquidation-price warnings. |
| Rug pulls, scams and failed transactions on meme coins, with no domestic recourse | High (financial) | Warn and cap position size. No auto-buy on newly launched tokens. |
| Rule drift over the next four months (FX Act Dec 2026, crypto tax Jan 2027, travel rule Feb 2027, FSCMA Art. 166 Feb 2027, KOFIA credit floor) | Medium | Keep a rules table with effective dates in the software. Review it monthly. |
| Expectation risk: a 1000x target pushes users toward bold play, where ruin is above 99.9% | High | Show base rates and the 0.1% bound beside any target. Never market thousandfold returns. |
| Tax record-keeping (crypto tax possibly from 2027; overseas gains self-filed) | Low at this size | Log cost basis and disposals per lot from day one. |

| What will the November–December 2026 votes decide on crypto tax postponement, the Digital Asset Basic Act and the single-stock leverage cap? | Each changes what the software may offer or must log. |

## Police now treat prediction-market stakes as gambling

The decisive fact for this account is that Korea has already moved from blocking prediction markets to **prosecuting their users**. On 18 August 2026 the communications subcommittee of the 방송미디어통신심의위원회 ordered access to Polymarket blocked. It found that the site carries information aiding gambling under the Criminal Act and "similar acts" under the National Sports Promotion Act. Its reasoning rested on three points: a winner-takes-all payoff on events users cannot control, an operator that sets rules, settles trades and takes fees, and Korea-specific markets such as Seoul rainfall. It rejected Polymarket's defences that it had dropped Korean-language service, took no KRW and ran non-custodially on smart contracts ([Newsis](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20260818_0003752716); [Wikitree](https://www.wikitree.co.kr/articles/1153222)). The first-pass research said no Korean user faced a criminal case, and that is out of date. Gangwon Provincial Police's cyber unit **booked 26 Polymarket users**, identified through public blockchain records, and by mid-September had **referred 18 to prosecutors** on Art. 246 gambling charges. The police held that staking crypto on an uncertain outcome is gambling "regardless of the 'investment' label" ([Kangwon Ilbo](https://www.kwnews.co.kr/article/20260917501775); [The Block](https://theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-09-17-south-korean-police-charge-polymarket-users-415333); [Chosun Biz via Daum](https://v.daum.net/v/20260908104207366)). Reported stakes were large: KRW 17.6 billion in total, KRW 5.7 billion for the biggest bettor. No source says whether small stakes were pursued, and no court has ruled yet.

Polymarket's own documentation lists South Korea as **"close-only" on the frontend while leaving the API unrestricted** ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/geoblock)). That gap gives no legal cover, since the conduct, not the access path, is the offence. A VPN is not itself illegal under any Korean statute found, but it breaches Polymarket's terms and does not change the gambling analysis. Polymarket has blocked VPN IP ranges and applied KYC to large or fast-moving accounts since about May 2026 ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/polymarket-cracks-down-on-vpn-users-as-legal-pressure-intensifies-in-dozens-of-countries-2000765379)). The legal reach is settled by statute and precedent. Criminal Act Art. 3 applies the Act to Korean nationals abroad, and the Supreme Court held in 2002도2518 that gambling at a Nevada casino is not justified merely because it is lawful there ([CaseNote 2002도2518](https://casenote.kr/대법원/2002도2518)). Art. 246① carries a fine of up to KRW 10 million unless the gambling is "momentary amusement". **Habitual gambling under Art. 246② carries up to 3 years** ([law.go.kr](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkProc.do?lsClsCd=L&lsNm=%ED%98%95%EB%B2%95&joNo=024600000&mode=11)). A plan to trade repeatedly toward a 1,000x return, staking a meaningful share of a student's wealth, fits the factors courts weigh *against* momentary amusement. That reading is an inference, not a ruling.

Kalshi is subtler but not safer. QUANTGEN's 21 October benchmark notes said Kalshi restricts Korean users, citing a single news report, and that claim does not hold. Two copies of Kalshi's own Member Agreement, from October 2025 and February 2026, list **North Korea but not South Korea** in §VI. A tracker says the 1 October 2026 revision likewise omits it, though that revision could not be read directly ([Kalshi Member Agreement](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/kalshi-member-agreement.pdf); [Wayback 2026-02-22](https://web.archive.org/web/20260222050154/https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-member-agreement.pdf); [Coinperps](https://www.coinperps.com/learn/kalshi-restricted-countries-prediction-market-perps)). The same §VI makes members represent that they are not located where trading is "prohibited under Applicable Law". So absence from the list does not make a Korean eligible once Korean law prohibits the activity. Calling Kalshi merely "grey", as the first pass did, understates the risk, because the police reasoning against Polymarket users does not depend on crypto rails. There is a second, unresolved route to non-compliance: if Kalshi, a CFTC-regulated exchange, counts as an overseas derivatives market, then FSCMA Enforcement Decree Art. 184① requires a general investor to reach it **through a Korean broker**, which Kalshi is not ([law.go.kr Decree Art. 184](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkProc.do?lsClsCd=L&lsNm=%EC%9E%90%EB%B3%B8%EC%8B%9C%EC%9E%A5%EA%B3%BC%20%EA%B8%88%EC%9C%B5%ED%88%AC%EC%9E%90%EC%97%85%EC%97%90%20%EA%B4%80%ED%95%9C%20%EB%B2%95%EB%A5%A0%20%EC%8B%9C%ED%96%89%EB%A0%B9&joNo=018400000&mode=11)). Kalshi perps would give leverage, but they are open only to US-based KYC'd members ([Kalshi Help](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15357656-applying-for-perpetuals-access)). Robinhood's and Crypto.com's event contracts are US-only, and PredictIt limits eligibility to US citizens and resident aliens ([Bonus.com](https://www.bonus.com/prediction-markets/crypto-com/); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/prediction-markets/predictit-review/)).

Sports contracts are the most dangerous corner of the map. The Supreme Court held in **2022도6462** that a person in Korea who bets on an overseas sports site is punishable under National Sports Promotion Act Art. 48(3), with **up to 5 years or KRW 50 million**, even when a foreigner runs the site from abroad. That provision has no momentary-amusement proviso ([law.go.kr 2022도6462](https://law.go.kr/LSW/precInfoP.do?mode=0&precSeq=232585); [law.go.kr Art. 48](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkProc.do?lsClsCd=L&lsNm=%EA%B5%AD%EB%AF%BC%EC%B2%B4%EC%9C%A1%EC%A7%84%ED%9D%A5%EB%B2%95&joNo=004800000&mode=11)). A Kalshi or offshore contract on a KBO or EPL result pays out on a sports prediction, which sits close to the Art. 26① definition of a similar act. The lawful alternatives are small and capped. Betman, the only legal sports-betting channel, allows **KRW 100,000 per round**. The first pass put its minimum at KRW 1,000; the correct figure is **KRW 100 per game**, with KRW 1,000 units only for single-game purchases ([The Fact](https://news.tf.co.kr/read/toto/2184980.htm); [Sports Kyunghyang](https://sports.khan.co.kr/article/202302201513003)). Online Lotto is capped at **KRW 5,000 per person per draw**. The "weekdays only" mobile rule was a first-half-2026 trial condition, not a permanent one ([Daum/Sisa Journal](https://v.daum.net/v/20260206134735534); [SBS Biz](https://biz.sbs.co.kr/amp/article/20000290213)).

## Deposit walls of KRW 10–30 million block every regulated lever

Korea's retail-protection regime works through deposit thresholds and mandatory courses, and at KRW 42,000 every one of them binds. KRX futures and options need a broker-set deposit of at least **KRW 10 million for stage 1 and KRW 20 million for stage 2** (option writing and VKOSPI futures). They also need at least 1 hour of education and 3 hours of mock trading, and brokers tier the hours by experience and age, so an investor with no derivatives history typically faces 3 hours of education and 5 of mock trading ([Paxnet guide](https://www.paxnet.co.kr/futures/guide); [FSC 2019 plan](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/73717); [Samsung guide](https://www.samsungpop.com/ux/kor/trading/giftoption/cmelinkgiftglobalmarket/futureOptionGuide.pop); [BizWatch](https://news.bizwatch.co.kr/article/market/2025/11/17/0025)). Domestic 2x ETPs need KRW 10 million plus a course. After the single-stock leveraged products listed on 27 May 2026 swung wildly, the FSC raised their deposit to **KRW 30 million in cash only**, effective 31 July. It added a 3-hour course and, from 19 August, 5 hours of KRX mock trading over 5 trading days, and halted new listings ([FSC via KDI EIEC](https://eiec.kdi.re.kr/policy/materialView.do?num=284654); [MTN](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2026071616232262750); [Economist Korea](https://economist.co.kr/article/view/ecn202608120061)). ELWs need KRW 15 million ([Asiae 2026-08-19](https://core.asiae.co.kr/article/2026081913544214024)). FX margin needs **US$10,000 of initial margin per 100,000-unit lot**, about 330 times this account ([Kiwoom](https://www.kiwoom.com/h/foreign/fofx/VFxMainView)). CFDs are reserved for individual professional investors. Since September 2023 that status requires, among other things, a month-end average balance of at least KRW 300 million for a year in the last five, with a 40% margin floor ([FSC 2023-07-19](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/80432)).

The most consequential correction concerns US-listed leveraged ETPs. The first-pass research said they needed only a 1-hour course and no deposit. **That was wrong.** Following an FSC plan of 30 January 2026, KOFIA amended its business rules on 10 April. Since **22 May 2026**, first-time buyers of overseas leveraged ETF/ETNs such as TQQQ or SOXL must hold a **KRW 10 million basic deposit**. Prior holders are exempt, and -1x inverse products are excluded ([Herald 2026-05-19](https://www.heraldk.com/article/2026051918101764619); [FSC](https://fsc.go.kr/eng/pr010101/86182); [Kakao Pay Securities notice](https://www.kakaopaysec.com/customer/notice/dynamicBoardPageDetail.do?id=7216)). The KRW 30 million single-stock rule also covers overseas-listed single-stock products, including additional buys ([FSC](https://fsc.go.kr/eng/pr010101/87405)).

What remains reachable is unlevered or nearly so. KOSPI and KOSDAQ shares move at most **±30% a day** (KONEX ±15%; KONEX's own deposit was abolished in 2022), with volatility-interruption auctions and a **0.20% sell-side tax in 2026** ([eToday](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/1127429); [FSC KONEX](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/77723); [Segye](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20260105516769)). Fractional trading from KRW 1,000 now reaches high-priced KOSPI200 and KOSDAQ150 names at several brokers ([SBS Biz](https://biz.sbs.co.kr/amp/article/20000338432)). Credit buying is lawful but caps at about **2.2x**, because the minimum deposit rate is 45%, not 40% as the first pass said, and KOFIA plans to raise it to 50%. KOFIA abolished the old KRW 1 million credit-account minimum in 2015, so access now turns on broker screening ([SBS Biz 2026-09-23](https://biz.sbs.co.kr/amp/article/20000336506); [Metro Seoul 2015](https://www.metroseoul.co.kr/article/2015040100149)). Retail short selling resumed on 31 March 2025 with 105% collateral, which gives less than 1x exposure and at most a 100% gain per position ([FSC 2025-03-21](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/84216)).

US stocks and ETFs through a Korean broker are plainly lawful at 1x. The law expects this route: FX Transaction Regulation Art. 7-33④ and Decree Art. 184① both direct general investors to a 투자중개업자 ([BOK](https://www.bok.or.kr/portal/main/contents.do?menuNo=200405); [Ilgan NTN](https://www.intn.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=2034122)). The first pass listed US options availability as unknown; Korean brokers in fact offer US single-stock and ETF options to retail. Samsung's service is buy-to-open and sell-to-close only. KB charges US$5.90 a contract online, with promotions at US$0.99. Toss appears to have options live in 2026, long-only with no exercise ([Samsung notice](https://www.samsungpop.com/ux/kor/customer/notice/notice/noticeViewContent.do?MenuSeqNo=20804); [KB notice](https://m.kbsec.com/go.able?linkcd=s060300010000&seq=10007347&idt=20240805); [Wolyo](https://www.wolyo.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=316719)). After the mandatory 1-hour course and 3 hours of mock trading, one contract fits in US$30 only if its premium is below about **US$0.24** at list commission. Direct accounts at Interactive Brokers are grey and lean non-compliant: the FX rules bind the resident directly, and unreported capital transactions carry fines of up to **KRW 100 million** ([law.go.kr FX Act Art. 32](https://www.law.go.kr/법령/외국환거래법/제32조); [IBKR KRX page](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/trading/krx-exchange.php)). Moomoo's official country list omits Korea ([Moomoo](https://www.moomoo.com/us/learn/moomoo-available-countries)).

## Crypto supplies the only leverage, through grey channels that are closing

Registered Korean exchanges offer spot only. The one margin-like product, coin lending, has been capped at collateral value since the **FSC/DAXA guideline of 5 September 2025**. It is open only for top-20 coins or coins on three or more KRW markets, after a DAXA course and an aptitude test ([FSC 2025-09-05](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/85247)). Upbit's own documentation confirms a **KRW 5,000 minimum order** in the KRW market, so this account supports about eight minimum orders. Upbit's KRW fee is 0.05%, and Bithumb's is 0.04% with a coupon ([Upbit docs](https://docs.upbit.com/kr/docs/krw-market-info); [Smart Today](https://www.smarttoday.co.kr/ko-kr/articles/110976)). The Digital Asset Basic Act, which could eventually permit domestic crypto derivatives, was still unpassed in late September, and the FSC was aiming for a November subcommittee ([Newspim](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20260923000764)).

Offshore venues are being squeezed from three sides. Operators serving Koreans without FIU registration face up to 5 years in prison, and the FSC has warned that influencers who promote them for referral fees may face criminal liability ([FSC 2026-06-24](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/87177)). On 13 October 2026 the regulator blocked **27 exchange websites**, including KuCoin, MEXC, BingX, Phemex, XT.com, Bitrue, CoinW, Poloniex, BTCC and DigiFinex. The first pass also named BitMart, but the Korean reports read do not ([Blockmedia](https://www.blockmedia.co.kr/archives/1150776); [SBS Biz](https://biz.sbs.co.kr/amp/article/20000339576)). Under a Google Play policy in force since 28 January 2026, OKX and Bybit can no longer be newly installed from the Korean store ([BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/84ec048d-c755-4cb1-9e6a-0d38fddb7920)). OKX's own disclosure restricts **derivatives and P2P for South Korea**, so offshore perps cannot be treated as one grey bucket ([OKX disclosure](https://www.okx.com/help/risk-compliance-disclosure)). No penalty for merely holding an account was found, and the Supreme Court held in 2024도10710 that an ordinary user trading for their own account is not a VASP. The same ruling, however, makes anyone who repeatedly trades or transfers crypto **for others for a fee** an unregistered VASP ([Segye](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20241229510093)). Any pooled-money or copy-trading feature is therefore off the table. Funding routes are also contested. The FIU sanctioned Bithumb on 16 March 2026 over 45,772 transactions with unregistered overseas VASPs, imposing a KRW 36.8 billion fine and restricting new customers' external transfers. That sanction is stayed pending litigation, and Dunamu won its first-instance case in April ([MTN](https://news.mtn.co.kr/article/2026102222290942305); [Herald](https://www.heraldk.com/article/2026042922291956837)).

DEXs are where leverage is technically reachable. Hyperliquid's terms, last updated 15 June 2026, restrict US persons, Ontario and sanctioned jurisdictions in **§1.6** (the first pass cited §1.5) and do not name Korea. They ban VPN circumvention, and §1.7 shuts outcome markets to users for whom they would be unlawful. Official margin tiers allow **40x on BTC, 25x on ETH, 20x on SOL and XRP and 10x on most others**, with base perp fees of 0.045% taker and 0.015% maker ([Hyperliquid ToS](https://app.hyperliquid.xyz/terms); [margin tiers](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/margin-tiers); [fees](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees)). pump.fun's terms, updated 17 October 2026, list the UK, North Korea and sanctioned states as prohibited but **not South Korea**. They require users to have reached the age of majority, which is 19 in Korea ([pump.fun Terms](https://pump.fun/docs/terms-and-conditions)). The first pass said DEXs were the only source of leverage above 1x, which overstates it, since Binance and Bybit futures reached by same-name transfer are another grey route. "No Korean rule bans DEX use" means only that none was found, not that use is permitted. The Virtual Asset User Protection Act's manipulation bans bind "anyone". Its penalty is **imprisonment of at least one year *or* a fine of 3–5x the profit**, not both by default as the first pass stated ([Art. 19](https://casenote.kr/법령/가상자산_이용자_보호_등에_관한_법률/제19조)).

These grey channels are narrowing on a fixed calendar. The first pass called the travel-rule effective date conflicting. The FSC resolves it: only the registration provisions took effect on 20 August 2026, and the travel-rule expansion applies six months after promulgation, about **18 February 2027** ([FSC 2026-08-11](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/87499); [m-economynews](https://www.m-economynews.com/mobile/article.html?no=69656)). Until then the KRW 1 million threshold still applies.

## A thousandfold month is a 0.1% lottery at best

The arithmetic is unforgiving. 1,000x is **9.97 doublings**. It needs **+38.9% compounded every KRX session** across the 21 sessions between 23 October and 20 November, or +25.9% every calendar day for 24/7 crypto. If every available bet has zero or negative expected value after costs, and losses stop at the account balance, wealth is a non-negative supermartingale. Then the probability of ever reaching 1,000 times the stake is **at most 1/1,000**, whatever the strategy: martingales, pyramiding, options and perps all included. The first pass omitted one condition. The bound requires limited liability, so recourse leverage such as overseas futures can "beat" it only by adding the risk of owing money, not by adding edge. In a subfair game the best a trader can do is bold play: stake everything needed to reach the goal on each bet ([Schweinsberg, arXiv math/0412362](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0412362)). Exact bold-play success probabilities for a 1,000x goal on even-money bets are **0.082% at a 49% win rate, 0.035% at 45% and 0.011% at 40%**, so ruin exceeds 99.9%. Even a genuine 52.5% edge reaches the goal under Kelly sizing only after about 5,500 bets, not in a month.

Every empirical base rate points the same way. Kalshi takers lose about **32%** of what they stake on average and makers about 10%, with a clear favourite-longshot bias ([CEPR/VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/economics-kalshi-prediction-market); [Bürgi, Deng and Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). That implies a 1,000:1 longshot hits about 0.068% of the time at best. Retail 0DTE traders lost about **US$358,000 a day** after daily expiries began, more than 60% of it to transaction costs ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-04-21/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options)). Lottery-like stock options underperform by 10–50% a week across skewness portfolios ([Boyer and Vorkink](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v69y2014i4p1485-1527.html)). **74–89% of EU retail CFD accounts lose money** ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933)), and **87.7% of Indian retail equity-derivatives traders lost in FY26** ([FOW](https://www.fow.com/insights/nearly-9-out-of-10-retail-equity-derivatives-traders-lose-money-in-fy26-sebi)). Korean retail lost about **KRW 458 billion a year on overseas derivatives** from 2020 through the first half of 2025, with no profitable year ([Asiae](https://core.asiae.co.kr/article/2025111417182638345); [Kyunghyang](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202511161310001/)). Of 204,004 Korean retail accounts studied in 2020, 46% lost money, and **62% of new investors** did ([fnnews](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202104131912345956)). On pump.fun about 1.4% of tokens ever graduate to a DEX. The first pass described a July 2026 spike to 6.7% as a week; it was a single day ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/pumpfun-launched-42000-tokens-in-one-day-fewer-than-2-will-ever-reach-a-dex); [The Block](https://theblock.co/post/409815/pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-jumps-boost-changes-launch-incentives)). **49 of the 50 most profitable pump.fun wallets were bots**, so the visible winners are mostly automated snipers, not replicable retail strategies ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/331108/business-memes-inside-solana-meme-coin-pump-fun?amp=1)).

Applied to the lawful options, the bound turns into near-zero odds. On KRX cash shares, 21 straight limit-up sessions give 1.3^21 ≈ **247x**, and 1,000x would need about 26.3 of them. That is not a strict ceiling: IPO-day trading spans 60–400% of the offer price, and limit-down to limit-up round trips exist. But surveillance designations halt trading after runs such as a 40% two-day rise on a warning-designated stock, so the practical probability is essentially zero ([KIND](https://kind.krx.co.kr/external/2026/07/15/000716/20260715001881/68818.htm)). Domestic crypto spot would need a registered-exchange coin to rise about 1,000x within the month, well below 0.01%. Long far-out-of-the-money US options are the most convex lawful path, but a commission of US$0.99–5.90 is **3–20% of the account per side**, and lottery options are systematically overpriced. A defensible estimate is 0.01–0.03%, below the 0.1% ceiling. A Lotto first prize does exceed 1,000x the ticket. At the online cap of five games a draw, a month of draws gives about **1 chance in 407,000**, because each game's odds are 1 in 8,145,060. The grey DEX routes sit under the same 0.1% ceiling with negative expected value after fees and liquidations. The only realistic forecast for any path is a loss of most of the KRW 42,000.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question TRADEGEN should be answering. The binding constraint is not which venue offers the most leverage. It is that Korean law now prices leverage by **deposit size and course completion**, and criminal law reaches **the act of staking on an outcome** wherever the venue sits. The gap between "a platform lets me in" and "I may lawfully trade there" has become the main design hazard: Polymarket's open API, Kalshi's missing Korea entry and Hyperliquid's silence on Korea are all platform facts, and none of them changes the Korean-law analysis. A defensible TRADEGEN for this user is a 1x, own-funds, single-user tool on registered domestic venues and Korean brokers. It should treat grey crypto routes as warned, size-capped options with dated kill-switches, and hard-block prediction markets and anything touching sports outcomes. Software that markets a path from $30 to $30,000 promises something the mathematics allows at most one time in a thousand, and the evidence suggests closer to one in ten thousand.

The second implication is temporal. Between now and February 2027, the FX Act, the travel-rule decree, the crypto tax, the FSCMA OTC amendment and the first Polymarket court rulings will each redraw part of this matrix. A compliance table hard-coded today will be wrong within four months unless it carries effective dates and a review cadence.
