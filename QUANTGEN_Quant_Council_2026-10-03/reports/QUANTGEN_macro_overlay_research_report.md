## 2026 turned into a real-yield, oil-shock and Korean-leverage regime

The macro backdrop on 2 Oct 2026 is a sequence of three regimes. A model calibrated on any one of them would have mis-specified the next. From January 2024 to mid-2025, disinflation and Fed easing were interrupted by short policy shocks: the 5 Aug 2024 yen-carry unwind and the April 2025 tariffs. From mid-2025 to June 2026, an AI and memory-chip melt-up lifted KOSPI **+197%**, from 30 Jun 2025 to its 22 Jun 2026 closing peak, and a war-driven energy shock arrived on top of it. Since June 2026 the world has been re-tightening: the ECB, BoJ, BoK and Fed all hiked, Korea's leverage unwound, and long-end term premia jumped (local: `T1_events.csv`).

### US labour cooled for two years, then re-tightened without a recession

Unemployment rose from **3.7% (Jan 2024) to a 4.5% peak in November 2025** (first print 4.6%, with payrolls +64k), then fell back to **4.1% in July–August 2026** ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_12162025.htm); [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.htm)). In the current vintage, payroll growth averaged 121.6k a month in 2024, 9.7k in 2025 (the weakest non-recession year in the sample) and 80.4k in January–August 2026. Six months between January 2025 and February 2026 were negative. The vacancies-to-unemployed ratio fell from 1.36 to 0.87 in December 2025 before recovering to 1.01 (local: `A1_labor_dashboard.csv`). The July 2024 print, at 4.3% with +114k jobs ([JPMorgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/markets-and-economy/economy/jobs-report-july-2024)), triggered the real-time **Sahm rule at 0.53**, rising to 0.57 in August. No recession has followed in the 26 months since, and the NBER still lists April 2020 as the last trough ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SAHMREALTIME); [NBER](https://www.nber.org/research/business-cycle-dating)).

Three data problems now define this labour picture, and each is a trap for any backtest. Payroll benchmarks were revised by a preliminary **−818k for 2024 and −911k for 2025**, the latter the largest since 2009; the finals were −598k (not seasonally adjusted) for 2024 and −898k (seasonally adjusted; −862k not seasonally adjusted) for 2025 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cesbmart.htm)) ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ces/notices/2024/2024-preliminary-benchmark-revision.htm); [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/prebmk_09092025.htm)). The real-time Sahm reading of 0.53 is 0.50 in the current vintage. And the 43-day shutdown meant **October 2025 CPI and household-survey data were never collected** ([Richmond Fed](https://www.richmondfed.org/research/national_economy/macro_minute/2025/phantom_figures_missing_data_in_october)). A positional `shift(12)` across that gap silently reports August 2026 CPI inflation as 3.71% instead of the correct 3.35% (local: T1-C5 check on `CPIAUCSL`).

### Inflation re-accelerated on energy, and the Fed reversed course

The Fed cut **175 bp between September 2024 and December 2025**, to 3.50–3.75%. It held through July 2026 while dissents migrated from doves to hawks: the April vote was 8–4 and the July vote 9–3, with three members voting to hike ([Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260429a.htm); [Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260729a.htm)). On **16 Sep 2026 it hiked 25 bp to 3.75–4.00%, by 12–0**, saying "Inflation remains elevated" and citing geopolitical uncertainty ([Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)). The median dot implies **4.1% at end-2026 and end-2027** ([Fed press conference](https://www.federalreserve.gov/mediacenter/files/FOMCpresconf20260916.pdf)). Kevin Warsh was confirmed as chair 54–45 on 13 May and sworn in on 22 May ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/13/nx-s1-5816235/kevin-warsh-federal-reserve-chair-jerome-powell)). Four months into his term the Fed hiked, falsifying the market prior that "the President's pick means cuts".

Headline CPI went from **2.4% in February 2026 to 4.2% in May and 3.4% in August**, with core at only 2.4% and gasoline up 27.4% year on year ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.htm)). Core PCE is 3.01%. Its three-month annualised rate was already 4.27% in February, before the war, so the core pickup was not purely oil (local: `A3_phillips_monthly.csv`). Seven Taylor-rule variants put the appropriate rate at 4.30–6.21%. Policy therefore sits **0.4–2.3 pp below the rules (central estimate −0.9 pp)**: the hike is rule-consistent in direction, and its size is not identified ([Fed Board policy rules](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/policy-rules-and-how-policymakers-use-them.htm); local: `A5_taylor_rules.csv`).

The BoK, BoJ and ECB also tightened into the energy shock, against the textbook advice to look through supply shocks, and the BoE came close. The **BoK** cut 100 bp to 2.50% (October 2024 to May 2025), then hiked to 2.75% on 16 Jul 2026 (unanimous) and **3.00% on 27 Aug 2026 by a 6–1 vote** ([UPI](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2026/07/16/bank-of-korea-rate-hike/9471784238480/); [BoK](https://www.bok.or.kr/eng/bbs/E0000634/view.do?nttId=11064193&oldMenuNo=400007&menuNo=400069&programType=newsDataEng&depth=400069&relate=Y)). The **BoJ** went from negative rates to **1.25% on 18 Sep 2026**, a 31-year high ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/18/japans-interest-rate-hiked-to-31-year-high-at-1-25-as-inflation-rises)). The **ECB** hiked from 2.00% to 2.25% on 11 Jun and **2.50% on 10 Sep** ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp260910~314e508016.en.html)). The BoE held at 3.75% with three votes to hike ([BoE](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/september-2026)).

### The long end broke out on real yields and term premium, not inflation expectations

The 10-year Treasury closed at **5.29% on 30 Sep 2026, the highest close since 14 May 2002** and above the 2007 peak of 5.26%. The "highest in about 19 years" description that circulated applies to the 5.0–5.26% levels, not to 5.29% (local: `DGS10`). The 2-year is at 4.88%, the 30-year at 5.64%, and the 10-year TIPS yield at 2.93%, its highest since November 2008. The Kim–Wright term premium is 1.02%, its first reading at or above 1.0 since 2010. Year to date, the 10-year rose 111 bp, of which **TIPS +100 bp and breakevens only +11 bp**. The Kim–Wright split is +45 bp term premium and +54 bp expected path (local: `A7_rates_windows.csv`).

This is the second time in two years that the expectations hypothesis failed for long yields. After the September 2024 first cut, the 10-year *rose* **113 bp while the Fed cut 100 bp**, with the term premium up 76 bp. That was the largest post-first-cut rise in seven easing cycles; the other six ranged from −67 to +42 bp. On 19 Aug 2026 the Treasury announced it would double long-end liquidity buybacks from 9 September ([Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0607)). Stocks and bonds now fall together. The 63-day stock–bond correlation is **+0.53**, and over 2021–26 bonds rallied by **0.0 bp** on the worst 5% of equity days, against +6.4 bp in 2000–20 (local: `A8_corr_by_year.csv`).

### The Hormuz oil shock was the largest supply disruption on record

US and Israeli strikes on Iran on 28 Feb 2026 cut Strait of Hormuz flows to **under 10% of roughly 20 mb/d**. The IEA's record 400 mb coordinated stock release on 11 March did not cap prices ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/news/iea-member-countries-to-carry-out-largest-ever-oil-stock-release-amid-market-disruptions-from-middle-east-conflict)); Dated Brent rose $11.4 the next day. Dated Brent spot went from **$61.35 at end-2025 to $126.69 at end-March and a peak of $138.21 on 7 Apr**. It fell to $70.46 by 30 June after a ceasefire, a US blockade and a June memorandum of understanding. It then rebounded to $130.8 on 15 Sep and **$113.96 on 29 Sep**, as the memorandum's vague strait language collapsed into a tanker war ([Columbia CGEP](https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/us-israeli-attacks-on-iran-and-global-energy-impacts/); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/28/oil-prices-surge-after-trump-rejects-irans-plan-to-reopen-strait-of-hormuz)).

The often-quoted $127 and $114 are **spot prices**. Front-month futures peaked at $118.35 on 31 March and stood near $103 on 1 Oct, and the spot–futures gap reached about **$29 in April**. Yahoo's continuous Brent future also prints a −14.5% "move" on 1 Apr 2026 that is mostly a contract roll (local: `r3_chair_P_checks.py`). Twenty days into the shock, the 2026 damage was far larger than in 2022: KOSPI −15.5% vs +0.4%, USD/KRW +5.2% vs +2.1%, and the 10-year real yield +32 bp vs −4 bp. The 5-year breakeven rose only 17 bp, and gold *fell* 13.8% (local: `A10_oil_shock_event_paths.csv`).

### KOSPI doubled on memory chips, lost 38.6% in 27 sessions, and the won rallied anyway

Korea moved from political crisis to boom to crash in under two years. KOSPI fell 9.6% in 2024, a year that ended with the 3 Dec martial-law shock. It rose **+75.6% in 2025** on governance reform and the memory super-cycle, and **+101.1% in H1 2026**, closing at a peak of **9,114.55 on 22 Jun 2026**. That happened while foreigners were net sellers of **₩148.3tn** and retail investors bought single-stock leveraged ETFs, listed from May 2026 ([KEIA](https://keia.org/analysis/explaining-south-koreas-stock-market-boom/)). Samsung and SK hynix reached **55.5% of KOSPI market cap**, against 22% at end-2023, and supplied about two-thirds of the 2025–26 index-point move (local: `B3_samsung_hynix_attribution.csv`; [Sedaily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/05/27/samsung-sk-hynix-surge-alone-as-chip-duo-tops-half-of-kospi)).

The unwind was structure-amplified. KOSPI fell a record **12.06% on 4 Mar** ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10686893)) and **9.99% on 23 Jun**, a day on which retail investors bought a record ₩8.54tn ([Sedaily](https://en.sedaily.com/markets/2026/06/23/kospi-plunges-10-percent-on-chip-concentration-leverage)). July brought four circuit breakers, 15 sidecars and the margin call of a leveraged AI hedge fund, the last a single-source T2 report ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/leopold-aschenbrenners-ai-hedge-fund-174426079.html)). The index troughed at **5,593.56 on 30 Jul**, then posted a record **+17.91% day on 31 Jul** on hyperscaler capex reaffirmations ([Sedaily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/07/31/kospi-whipsaws-18-percent-plunge-over-three-days-then-18)).

From 22 Jun to 30 Jul, KOSPI fell 38.6% while the S&P 500 fell 0.5%, TAIEX 16.4% and the Nikkei 14.5% (15.1% to its 29 Jul low). The same AI shock thus produced about 2.4× the drawdown in Korea. Q3 closed **−19.3%** (not the −18.8% sometimes quoted), and KOSPI closed at 7,003.74 on 2 Oct.

The won broke the textbook link. USD/KRW went from 1,477.9 at end-2024 to 1,555.96 on 5 Jun 2026, then **fell to 1,356.5 by 25 Sep**: −12.8% from that high and −12.4% over Q3, while the broad dollar fell only 0.5% and foreigners sold ₩21.5tn of Korean equities in September. Record exports, **$120.9bn in September (+83.5%)** with chips above $60bn, together with BoK hikes and the NPS swap line, made the won behave like an exporter-earnings currency rather than a risk proxy ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/economy/20261001/koreas-monthly-exports-hit-1209-bil-in-sept-on-record-chip-sales)). The yen is near 157 after two solo interventions in 2026 and a joint US–Japan intervention on 30–31 Jul, the first since 2011 ([MoF](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/public_relations/statement/others/20260803073000.html)). Bitcoin fell 53% from its October 2025 peak to $58.6k on 30 Jun 2026, including −33% in H1 2026 while the war was under way, and has rebounded to about $84.8k ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/06/05/bitcoin-loses-usd60-000-falls-to-weakest-price-since-october-2024); local: `CBBTCUSD`).

## Textbooks teach the mechanisms and the econometric failure tools; courses add the asset-pricing puzzles

The professors mapped the attached textbooks and about 20 university courses onto the macro-to-markets channels. They found a clean division of labour.

**Textbook macro supplies the mechanisms.** Lee & Kim's *Dynamic Macroeconomics* (Korea University slides; local) covers Korean cycle facts (ch.1), growth (ch.2–5), RBC (ch.6), money (ch.7), the New Keynesian Phillips curve (ch.8), and NK-DSGE with Taylor rules and commitment versus discretion (ch.9). Blanchard & Johnson 6e (local) adds Okun's law, the expectations-augmented Phillips curve with its 1.8 sacrifice ratio, the term structure and stock pricing (ch.15), and the open-economy models of UIP, Mundell–Fleming and sudden stops. Kirchgässner–Wolters–Hassler (local) supplies the econometric toolkit: VAR, Granger causality, unit roots, cointegration and GARCH. Dutta (local) gives the game theory of commitment, OPEC-style cartels, tariffs and wars of attrition.

**The textbooks already contain the econometric failure tools** (structural breaks, spurious regression and forecast evaluation in Kirchgässner–Wolters–Hassler §2.4.3 and §5.3.4) and the Lucas critique (Blanchard & Johnson p.176); **finance courses add the asset-pricing puzzles**, and the overfitting and forecast-comparison toolkit comes from the research literature, not from courses. Cochrane's free Booth/Hoover class has modules on time-series predictability and on "Equity Premium, Macroeconomics, Asset Pricing" ([Cochrane](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/asset-pricing)). Princeton's ECO 529 centres risk-bearing capacity and intermediary asset pricing ([Princeton](https://markus.scholar.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf2651/files/documents/00eco529_syllabus_f2022_a.pdf)). The Stanford/Columbia international-finance syllabus (undated; no course number) teaches Meese–Rogoff, carry and CIP deviations as standing puzzles ([Maggiori–Schreger](https://globalcapitalallocation.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/International_Macro+_Finance_Syllabus.pdf)). Berkeley's monetary lecture treats the 2021–23 deviation from the Taylor rule as the Fed falling "behind the curve" ([Steinsson L17](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~jsteinsson/teaching/Lecture17MonetaryPolicy.pdf)). Oxford's MFE notes provide the most directly usable layer for QUANTGEN: bootstrap, GARCH, realised variance and VaR/ES backtests ([Sheppard](https://www.kevinsheppard.com/teaching/mfe/notes/)).

One correction matters for self-study. MIT 14.02 now assigns **Blanchard 8e**, not the 6e on GEON's shelf, so courses should be mapped by chapter title ([MIT OCW](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-02-principles-of-macroeconomics-spring-2023/pages/syllabus/)). Korea University's published course descriptions cover macro dynamics (ECON 359), money and banking (ECON 331), international financial economics (ECON 461) and time-series econometrics (ECON 242). That document is undated. The dated 2026-1 undergraduate listing adds Financial Economics (ECON 354), Intermediate Econometrics (ECON 463), Behavioral Finance (ECON 465) and Korean Macroeconomic Analysis (ECON 439), but **no systematic-trading course**; ECON 49x is not on the public pages (UNVERIFIED) ([KU](https://econ2.korea.ac.kr/econ/community/data.do?mode=download&articleNo=351416&attachNo=257175&totalNoticeYn=&totalBoardNo=); [KU 2026-1](https://econ2.korea.ac.kr/econ/intro/lecture_under.do?mode=view&articleNo=804857&article.offset=0&articleLimit=10&totalNoticeYn=N&totalBoardNo=)); KAIST's MFE does, in FE537 Statistical Arbitrage and FE547 Algorithmic Trading ([KAIST](https://www.business.kaist.edu/download/mfe/KCB_MFE_e.pdf)). The macro-to-trading bridge must therefore come from outside the KU core. The panel's recommended order of study is: volatility econometrics, then out-of-sample predictability hygiene, then strategy families, then monetary-event reading, then FX, and macro-finance theory last.

## Where the models work, and where they broke between 1960 and 2026

The models fail in three recurring ways.

**Structural breaks.** Relations estimated under one policy regime die under the next, exactly as the Lucas critique in B&J (p.176) warns. Okun broke around 2011. The Phillips curve flattened after 1985 and vanished after 2008, consistent with anchored expectations ([Hazell et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28005)). The 1.8 sacrifice ratio implied about 5.5 pp-years of excess unemployment for the 2023–25 disinflation; the realised figure was −1.57, with unemployment *below* its natural rate (local: `A3_phillips_fits.csv`).

**Lateness and revisions.** Recession signals arrive after the fact or get revised. The Sahm rule caught 9 of 9 recessions since 1960, firing a median **two months after** they began (range four months before to three months after). NBER announced the April 2020 trough about 15 months later. Chauvet–Piger's real-time model calls peaks about a month after NBER's own announcement ([Chauvet–Piger](https://jeremypiger.com/assets/files/Chauvet_Piger_2008_JBES.pdf)).

**Regime-conditional signs.** Some signs hold only within a regime and flip without warning. The dollar's haven bid failed in April 2025, the stock–bond correlation turned positive with inflation, and the won rallied during a foreign sell-off.

Underneath all three sits a cleaner empirical law that both panels adopted. **Implied volatility and credit-spread changes carry incremental out-of-sample variance information for US horizons up to a month, and no stress variable forecasts lower returns out of sample** (none survives a BH-10% correction, EPU included). The VIX level forecasts US variance and the VIX/VIX3M slope adds a little: t = 7.17 (R² 0.385) on its own, about 4 given current realised volatility, against t = 0.04 for next-21-day returns. Neither is a KOSPI volatility input beyond KOSPI's own volatility. Across 22 episodes since 1988 in which Brent rose 30%, forward 63-day returns were 2.67% against 2.66% unconditional. Weeks with the dollar and equities both down were followed by **+7.27%** over 13 weeks, against 2.84% normally (local: `r2_P2_p3_checks.py`, `r2_P2_p1_checks.py`, `r2_P3_p4_checks.py`).

What survives this record is short and consistent. **Own-volatility scaling of momentum** works because momentum's risk is persistent and forecastable while its mean is not. Over the full sample it lifts the Sharpe ratio in every region. Under the spec's 1.0× cap the cap binds in 20 of 37 years, and in those years the scaled sleeve had the higher Sharpe in 15 (75%) gross and 11 (55%) net of 50 bp; the other 17 years are ties, so the gain is concentrated in crash years. Under the spec's rule z is 3.84 / 3.29 / 2.99 with 10- / 63- / 240-session blocks, so it clears the one-sided ≤ 8-cell hurdle (2.50) at every block length but the one-sided Bonferroni-120 hurdle (3.34) only with short blocks; the research rule (cap 2, gross) gives 4.83 / 4.31 / 3.93 ([Barroso–Santa-Clara](https://novaresearch.unl.pt/en/publications/momentum-has-its-moments/); [Moreira–Muir](https://amoreira2.github.io/alan-moreira.github.io/VolPortfolios_published.pdf)). The decay warning is real, though. Its local gain (with a 2× leverage cap) fell from +0.66 in 1990–2011 to +0.26 in 2012–26, and Cederburg et al. find that 103 vol-managed strategies "do not systematically outperform" ([Cederburg et al.](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/on-the-performance-of-volatility-managed-portfolios/)).

In the published paper the **Daniel–Moskowitz** dynamic strategy adds about +0.15 on top of constant-volatility scaling (Table 7: 1.041 → 1.194). QUANTGEN's bear-state gate is a floored binary heuristic inspired by it, not the published rule, and its expected add-on is about +0.07 (local: +0.072 net of 50 bp). Locally, it misses crashes that follow bull markets: Nov-2020 (−12.0%), Jul-2026 (−10.9%) and Jan-2001 (−21.9%) ([Daniel–Moskowitz JFE](https://www.kentdaniel.net/papers/published/jfe_16.pdf)).

**Hedge validity** is a state, not a constant. Bonds failed to hedge equities in 2022, April 2025 and Q3 2026, and the dollar failed in April 2025.

## How the panels argued: statistics versus survival, and both changed their minds

The professor panel had six members: macro theory (P1), time-series econometrics (P2), asset pricing and factors (P3), game theory and policy (P4), curricula (P5) and quant ML methods (P6). The executive panel had seven: cross-asset strategist (E1), NYSE trader (E2), FX/rates trader (E3), Asia/EM portfolio manager focused on Korea (E4), CRO and systematic PM (E5), IB vice-chair (E6) and global head of research (E7). In round 2, each member cross-examined colleagues *within* the same panel. Reviewers had to give every claim a verdict, re-open the sources of at least eight key numbers per paper, and recompute at least three of them from local data. Each chair then issued a resolution. The professors' chair produced 30 consensus statements, 11 contested and 32 refuted or withdrawn. The executives' chair produced 30, 11 and 25, plus a BUILD NOW / SHADOW / BACKLOG / DROP triage.

The fault line ran between statistical power and survival. The professors demanded multiplicity-adjusted evidence: z ≥ 2.7 with no more than 8 pre-registered cells, or z ≥ 3.35 against a Bonferroni-120 bar ([Harvey–Liu–Zhu](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2249314)). The executives accepted small, plausible de-risk effects and controlled the risk of being wrong through governance. The cross-asset persona's rebuttal to its own reviewer became the executive panel's motto: "**a one-person shop dies of tails, not of 0.09 Sharpe**". The two positions were reconciled by arithmetic everyone accepted, and the audit made it stronger. The chairs assumed overlay and base books about 0.98 correlated, giving a standard error of ΔSR of 0.034 for 36 years of US data and 0.052 for 11 years of KOSPI, and **33–44 years** to detect +0.05 (executive chair resolution, EC-E-02). That holds only for add-on legs. For the market vol targets (correlation 0.89–0.92 with the base) the standard error against a same-exposure static book is 0.061 (US) and 0.130 (KOSPI), so detecting a +0.05 gain would take about **150–330 years**; the minimum detectable effect at 80% power is 0.13 and 0.28. Every one of 26 tested overlays had a 95% confidence interval that straddled zero.

### The disputes that mattered, and how each resolved

**The Daniel–Moskowitz floor** split the panels in an unexpected direction. The asset-pricing professor argued for 0×. The risk officer argued for 0.5, on the ground that an overlay should never impose a hard zero. Q1b's numbers sided with the professor on Sharpe: +0.26 at 0× against +0.14 at 0.5×. The round-4 recomputation then showed that the floor-0.5 gate still adds **+0.084** on top of capped vol scaling, and +0.056 with 2008–09 removed (share 0.97). Its cost is concentrated in May–Nov 2008, when it earned +9.5% against +21.8% for the static book. Both chairs built it at 0.5 and sent 0.25 to the backlog.

**Reversal in fast stress** produced a role swap. The professors held gross data showing reversal earns 59.2% a year at 34.8% volatility on VIX-inverted days (Sharpe 1.70), against 1.8% at 12.3% otherwise. Mean-variance logic would therefore up-weight it in stress (μ/σ² 4.9 vs 1.2), which the spec's no-up-weight bound forbids. On that basis they said reversal should be held at 1.0× and *never cut*. The executives cited KRX price limits, 41 sidecars from 2 Jan to 24 Jul 2026 (about 30 in H1) and spreads widening about 2.5× in stress, and wanted it blocked. A read-only desk has no fill data, so the question goes to a forward-simulated test priced with KIS quote snapshots (BL-C02).

**The macro-conditioned overlay floor** pitted P1 against P6. P1 showed that own-market de-risk overlays earn more when core PCE is at least 3% (+0.14 vs +0.05) and proposed a floor of 0.5 in tightening regimes. P6 replied that every confidence interval contained zero and that macro would have mis-called 2026, when KOSPI rose 68% from 31 Mar to 30 Jun during the Fed's hawkish turn. The resolution split the verdict. P6 won the narrow question of daily equity gross. P1 won on scope: shock type determines which hedges work. The interaction is pre-registered for a locked 2027+ holdout.

**Korea discrete flags** showed how fragile local rules are. P6's proposed fix, "high vol AND below the 200-day average", removed the 2026 bull tax but also all Q3 protection (−38.1% vs −9.6%). P6 conceded: "exactly why I refused to tune it: every fix is a trial, and none beats buy-and-hold."

**The FX desk's KRW refinements** all failed: a two-speed gate (+0.002), a residual filter (−0.03) and a "dollar regime B" switch that was on for 1% of days and missed April 2025. The plainest rule survived instead.

**Oil** was argued out on the 2026 path. The Brent 20-day >+25% gate first fired on 6 Mar 2026, **two sessions after the −12.06% day**, and lowered Sharpe. The textbook AS-shock sign held for headline inflation but not for equities over the following quarter.

The **core links** cluster in four areas.

**Size by own risk, not by macro forecast.** Overlays are de-risk-only: multipliers stay within [0.5, 1.0], the vol-target weight is capped at 1.0, and only the fail-closed risk engine can go to 0×. Momentum gets own-vol scaling and the D&M gate floored at 0.5. Reversal and quality stay at 1.0×. Stress variables feed volatility estimates and new-entry blocks, never cut-and-re-add triggers.

**Korea is its own market.** Korea gets a local 20-day vol target capped at 1.0. If Samsung and SK hynix together exceed 45% of KOSPI, they are held to no more than 35% of the long book. US overnight moves enter only as opening-gap inputs: the prior US session jointly explains about 15% of KOSPI daily variance (about 10% from the S&P alone), and the SOX beta rose from 0.17 (2015–19) to 0.56 in 2026 (0.25 over the full 2015–26 sample).

**Data hygiene is the largest single source of fake edge.** Every input carries UTC and publication timestamps. Lags are calendar-based. Brent is always named as spot or futures. Closes are used, never circuit-breaker prints. Missing inputs fail closed.

**Macro as context and hedge-validity state.** Sahm, the curve probit, Taylor ranges, real yields and term premium are logged and never act. Hedge credit is withdrawn when core PCE is at least 3% or the 63-day stock–bond correlation is positive.

The **eleven prohibitions** cover every flagship of the prior design: upward multipliers and the four-state table; VIX-term-structure momentum switches; macro factor gates (curve, real rate, credit, levels); Korea gates built from US inputs; fixed textbook coefficients; the lagged oil gate; net-liquidity and M2 rules; regime and ML shortcuts (smoothed probabilities, unpenalised HMMs, "complexity" timing, LLM backtests before the model's training cutoff); Street forecasts and surveys as inputs; 24-hour blackouts; and calendar and issuance tilts.

That is consistent with Nagel's critique ([Nagel](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34104)).

## What this means for a read-only research desk

Spec v1.0 was written as if QUANTGEN routes capital; v1.1 re-maps it onto the real application, which does not. It is an open-source, read-only research desk with "no code path that sends an order" and a simulated order book. Its rules are "decide at the close of day t, earn from day t+1" and "the gate decides, not the author" (QUANTGEN public page and architecture review, local: `00_QUANTGEN_public_page_text.txt`). Its nine-step gate is fixed before each test. It runs a purged, embargoed 756/126-session walk-forward and requires a block-bootstrap Sharpe interval that clears zero, a deflated Sharpe of at least 0.95, and a probability-of-overfitting check. It then requires a one-sided p ≤ 0.10 against the benchmark, a positive Sharpe at twice the costs, a plateau rather than a peak, and at least 60% of years positive, and it reports expected live Sharpe at 60% of out-of-sample (a 40% haircut; `live_sharpe_multiplier: 0.60`). Translated onto that design, the panels' integration plan fits the desk's philosophy and exposes three concrete gaps.

The translation is direct. "BUILD NOW" means the rule is computed, displayed and allowed to size strategy cards in the Strategy Lab; in spec v1.1 only a card with a PASS verdict may feed the displayed recommendation, and the other BUILD NOW cards run as descriptive books labelled with their verdicts. "SHADOW" means the rule is computed and logged as a dated, pre-registered card with a forward record, and is *withheld* as a signal, which matches the desk's existing "Withheld" labels. "DO-NOT-BUILD" maps onto REJECT cards that stay on the public record. That makes the backlog's failure archive a natural extension of "it says no, in public". The existing Macro surface computes an 18-indicator sheet with z-scores and a regime read. It is the right home for the spec's context log (R-13), on two conditions. Its z-scores must use expanding or trailing windows with publication timestamps, never present-calibrated levels. And nothing in it may feed a signal: that is the panels' shared rule that slow macro is logged context (N-30), together with the rule against present-calibrated levels (N-12, EC-E-28). The same applies to the crypto page's six-part regime score.

The first gap is **the gate itself**. The nine-step gate is built to test claims of *outperformance*: step 5 requires a one-sided p ≤ 0.10 against buy-and-hold on the same days. The panels showed that a de-risk overlay's Sharpe gain cannot clear such a test in any sample QUANTGEN has. The desk already has the evidence. Its Strategy Lab card "E1 · trend switch" (unrelated to the E1 persona), a long-or-cash rule on US ETF sleeves, was rejected with an out-of-sample Sharpe of 0.84 against 0.95 and p = 0.75, even though it cut the drawdown from −27% to −15%: "It protects; it does not outperform, and the claim was outperformance". Every overlay both panels endorse would meet the same fate, including the Korea vol target, which turned −38.6% into −12.2%.

The consistent fix is a second, pre-registered claim type for risk control. It would be judged drawdown-first on ΔMDD, CVaR, time under water, worst-day coverage and re-entry lag. It would require Sharpe non-inferiority rather than superiority, and pass leave-one-crisis-out with no episode supplying more than half the benefit. It would also report the bull tax every month. This keeps "the gate decides", because the criteria are fixed before the test. Spec v1.1 §8.7 now defines exactly this class (CARD-P, checks PC-1..PC-7), alongside Tiers A/B/C, forward-simulated books and demotion rules. The desk's own 40% post-publication haircut points the same way as the decay the panels measured for vol scaling with a 2× cap, where the Sharpe gain fell from +0.66 to +0.26, though that decay was larger. The published *JF* version of McLean–Pontiff is now verified: across 97 predictors, portfolio returns are 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication, so post-publication returns are about 43% below the out-of-sample level. The desk's 40% cut therefore sits inside the published range and is a calibrated default, not a convention; each card should also print its own Harvey–Liu haircut ([McLean–Pontiff JF](https://tevgeniou.github.io/EquityRiskFactors/bibliography/AcademicReviewFactor.pdf); [ABFER abstract](https://www.abfer.org/component/edocman/main-annual-conference/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability)).

The second gap is **costs**. The panels and spec v1.0 assumed a 0.35% Korean round trip, with a 0.20% sell tax; spec v1.1 adopts the desk's own costs (see the Addendum). The desk pre-registers **35 bp per side for KRX stocks**, which is 70 bp a round trip and 140 bp in the 2× stress case. Every factor result in the body of this report is gross of costs; the Addendum gives the net values. The reversal family, whose stress premium is the most cost-sensitive finding, must therefore be re-tested at the desk's own cost before any Korean reversal card is believed. The same applies to the D&M gate on Korean stock momentum, which is untested on KRX data. The Korean literature finds 12-1 momentum negative in 2000–2019, with apparent profits that are a carry-over of 1–2-month reversal ([KJFS](https://www.e-kjfs.org/journal/view.php?number=901); [JDQS 2021](https://www.emerald.com/jdqs/article/29/3/174/206550/The-effect-of-short-term-return-reversals-on)).

The third gap is **data the desk already has but does not log**. The backlog's top item (BL-U01) is a Korea leverage, flow and semis-cycle state, which needs investor-type flows, margin balances and leveraged-ETF AUM. Its KRW leg has been decided on the existing history (a REJECT on 1997–2026), so a true-timestamp closing rate is now a low-priority audit item. The desk's KIS key already reads "**USD/KRW fixing, investor flows, short-sale data**". Logging these daily from now on, with publication timestamps, is the single cheapest step toward settling the most important open question. KRX margin debt and leveraged-ETF AUM still need a separate source, and a trading-volume proxy is no substitute: it fails out of sample.

Three further implications follow from the desk's current state.

**Quant Lab.** The GARCH(1,1)-t fan is already labelled REJECT on SPY in its t-quantile (fan-tail) form: VaR was breached on 6.6% of days and the PIT test failed at p 0.003 (the app's own study, UNVERIFIED until its script and seed are published). In the FHS form used for sizing, long-window GARCH-t and GJR-t pass on KOSPI and the US. KOSPI's 2026 regime, with 21-day realised volatility above 30% on 88% of days and single-day moves of +17.91% and −12.06%, sits inside today's 504-close window, which is the problem: a 504-close GARCH is captured by whatever regime fills it (persistence 0.27–1.00 across 303 monthly refits; the latest fit implies a 61% unconditional volatility), and 2026 will later roll out of it. R-16 therefore matters: parameters on an expanding window of at least ten years, tails by FHS/EVT, and acceptance on out-of-sample 99% VaR, ES 97.5% and fan-PIT tests. QUANTGEN should seed a named scenario library: the 2026 Hormuz path on Dated Brent *and* futures, the July 2026 KOSPI unwind, the September 2026 rates shock, the August 2024 carry unwind and April 2025 "sell America". Fed SEP error bands (±1.4 pp on the one-year policy rate in the December 2023 SEP, about a 70% band; a 90% fan needs about ±2.2 pp) can serve as uncertainty priors, rather than relying on a fitted fan whose left tail is known to be thin ([Fed SEP](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20231213.htm)).

**Rates & FX page.** The page already shows the Fama regression and withholds FX momentum and dollar carry as signals. That is exactly where the UIP evidence points: the Diebold–Li curve forecast beat a random walk at 0 of 5 maturities.

**Crypto.** The desk's two passing strategy cards, crypto trend on KRW-BTC and BTC-USD, match the panels' crypto rule of own trend plus volatility. The prohibited net-liquidity rule (−0.20 Sharpe in 2023–26) should be recorded as a REJECT card rather than built.

## Limitations: what this evidence cannot tell you

The data are mostly **latest-vintage**. FRED macro series include later revisions; only the real-time Sahm series is a true vintage. Macro-gate backtests therefore carry a look-ahead bias that ALFRED vintages would reduce, and Ghysels–Horan–Moench show real-time data "substantially" weaken macro predictive power ([GHM](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/oup/rfinst/v31y2018i2p678-714..html)).

There are **no KRX stock-level, investor-flow, margin or minute data** locally. The Korea leverage state, foreign-flow regimes, Korea factor priors, execution windows and surprise-to-volatility rules are therefore untested. (Overnight information has since been tested on vendor OHLC: the prior US session explains the KOSPI opening gap, R² 0.32, but not the open-to-close return, R² 0.003.) KOSPI's 2026 extremes come from Yahoo closes and should be reconciled with KRX official closes.

All factor tests in the body use **Ken French factors gross of costs**, and the desk's own costs are twice what the panels assumed. The Addendum gives the overlay-incremental net values; the factors' own constituent turnover is still uncosted.

Inference is **weak by construction**. The 63-day circular-block bootstrap is shorter than the 214–245-day Politis–White blocks suggested for squared returns ([Politis–White](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Politis_White_2004.pdf)). Longer blocks lower the D&M gate's z-statistic from 2.01 to 1.80. Every overlay ΔSR lies inside the envelope that is undetectable over 10–30 years; for the market vol targets a +0.05 gain would take about 150–330 years to detect. The 2022–26 period was dissected episode by episode, so it is a reported stress-test set, not a holdout. Many 2026 lessons rest on n = 1: the joint yen intervention, the mega-supply flag, the DRAM-guide deceleration and the margin-called AI fund.

The **panels are simulated personas**. Their disagreements are structured arguments over shared evidence, not independent expert surveys, and their convergence partly reflects that they read the same data labs.

A few chronology sources are **T3**. They are used only for event dating and are flagged below.

Spec v1.0's execution conventions — the 09:00–09:30 KST no-entry window, closing-auction routing and the BoK release windows — are moot for a read-only desk except as assumptions about which prices are tradable. Spec v1.1 drops closing-auction routing and keeps the windows only as evaluator blocks on simulated entries.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question QUANTGEN should ask of macro. The prior design asked *which macro state predicts returns*. Six decades of macro data, a century of factor data, two panels and one honest timestamp audit answered that nothing in QUANTGEN's sample does it reliably enough to detect. The productive question is *which state tells you the risk you are carrying, or the hedge you are counting on, has changed*. Framed that way, the most valuable "macro" inputs turn out not to be macroeconomic. They are a strategy's own volatility, Korea's own volatility and concentration, the sign of the stock–bond correlation, and a timestamp. The classic macro models survive as a reading list for interpreting central banks, not as code.

For a desk that publishes its refusals, this is an advantage. Most of the research output is a well-documented list of rules that fail, and those already have a home as REJECT cards. The one structural change it forces is in the gate. A desk built to test outperformance cannot evaluate risk control, so QUANTGEN needs a second, pre-registered, drawdown-first claim type before any overlay can earn a verdict. The most important unanswered question is local: what led Korea's 2026 deleveraging. The data to answer it are partly in QUANTGEN's KIS feed today, though not yet logged.

- **Momentum own-vol scaling is the only overlay with a clear net benefit,** and the only one likely to enter the displayed recommendation once its card runs on QUANTGEN's own sleeve.
